## 0. Why this plan exists, in one paragraph

TMT has an implementation and a ruler but no demonstrated long memory, and its
standing blocker is **gradient SNR ≈ 0.05** for the whole 889,897-step run. The
paper we just read says two things that bear directly on this: (a) byte models
start worse and end higher, so TMT's compute budget is far left of the point
where the byte thesis pays; (b) **distillation beats cross-entropy at matched
compute**, and the teacher's *distribution* carries more information per byte
than its text does. So: give TMT a teacher, and measure it the way the paper
warns you must — on two rulers, never on CE alone.

## 1. The one hard number (storage)

Measured by gate 0 on the ruler's own text: **4.106 bytes/token**
(`data/eval/tinystories_valid.txt`, 100 kB window; 4.011 / 4.039 on the other two).

```
teacher dump, k=32:  32 x fp16 value + 32 x int32 index = 192 B / token
                    192 / 4.106                        = 46.8 B / training byte
 64 MB slice  ->  3.0 GB       256 MB slice -> 12.0 GB
```

Top-k is mandatory, and **k=32 is sufficient up to byte depth ~2**: a k-sweep to
the full 64 k vocabulary moves retained prefix mass by 0.005 at byte depth 1 and
0.000 at depth 2 (`RESULTS.md`, 2026-10-03). k=8 is too small (costs 5 points at
depth 0).

**SCOPE CORRECTION 2026-10-04.** That sufficiency claim was measured at depths
1-2 only, which is where retained mass is *high* (0.798 / 0.291). The corrected
per-depth table shows the opposite at the tail: **depth >= 4 is mostly one-hot
under k=32** (6.1 % non-degenerate at depth 4, ~4 % at 5+, retained mass 0.169
and falling). So "k=32 is sufficient" is true for the band that carries the target
and false for the depths that degenerate; raising k would recover those at linear
storage cost and is **untested**. Do not cite the sufficiency claim without this
scope. See `RESULTS.md`, "The MI target was wrong at every depth >= 1".

Under top-k **neither MI nor EoT is exact**. But the
sentence that used to sit here — *"so the paper's headline EoT>MI gap is a
full-vocabulary effect and is not available to us anyway"* — was **wrong**, and
it is the reason this needs rewriting rather than deleting:

* EoT's `<eot>` channel recovers the mass of candidates that **end** at depth
  `d-1`, which MI drops by the `len(c) > d` filter. At any `k`, EoT's surviving
  set is a strict **superset** of MI's, so EoT is the more faithful conversion
  under truncation too. The gap does not evaporate at `k=32`; it shrinks.
* Part of EoT's win has nothing to do with vocabulary size at all. The paper
  (§2.2): *"the ~30.94% additional compute it spends per unit of data (one
  `<eot>` every ~4.5 bytes) turns out to further improve asymptotic performance
  relative to Marginalize-It."* That is a compute-per-unit effect, not a
  full-vocab effect.

**Why MI is still the right call here, on the real reasons:**

1. **Storage.** EoT needs the teacher's byte distribution at ~257 symbols per
   *byte* rather than `k` ids per *token*. Our dump budget (46.8 B/training
   byte, measured above) is the constraint that forced top-k in the first place;
   EoT does not fit the same budget.
2. **Student vocabulary.** EoT makes the student emit a 257th symbol and
   lengthens every sequence by ~22% (`4.5 → 5.5` units per token, paper Table 7).
   TMT's whole thesis is a fixed 256-byte vocabulary.
3. **Segmentation transfer.** MI's target is defined from the teacher's prefix
   structure without asking the student to model token boundaries, which is the
   reason that *does* survive truncation.

Point 3 is the one that was load-bearing before and still is; points 1-2 are new
and are the honest headline. If the storage budget ever moves, revisit EoT —
it is not foreclosed on fidelity grounds, only on budget grounds.

`tools/teacher_table.py` builds the table with **zero dependencies** so it can be
verified anywhere.

### Step 3 — off-policy MI distillation — **THE EXPERIMENT** (~1.5 h T4 + 10 min dump)

Corpus: `data/wikipedia_clean/wikistories/wiki_tinystories_00{1..4}.txt`, with
each file's **sha256 recorded in RESULTS.md** so both arms are provably
byte-matched. Three arms, same seed 0, same `--data-seed 0`, same file order:

| arm | objective | flag |
|---|---|---|
| A | CE only (existing path; must be bit-identical at α=0) | `--distill-alpha 0` |
| **C** | **half-CE control: α=0.5, all-zero target** | **`--distill-alpha 0.5 --distill-null-target`** |
| B | MI-KD, α = 0.5, τ = 1 | `--distill-alpha 0.5 --distill-dir …` |

**Arm C is not optional and did not exist until 2026-10-04.** With `α·KL +
(1−α)·CE`, `α=0.5` halves the CE weight at *every* position while the KD term
fires only where a target exists. A "KD arm" is therefore not "CE +
distillation", it is "half-strength CE everywhere plus a soft label on a
subset". Both 2026-10-03 A/Bs are uninterpretable without arm C: their
divergence is consistent with the halved CE alone. Any future KD arm reports
A vs **C** before it reports A vs B.

Dump input is the same corpus, bucketed with `numpy_port.Runtime.make_streams`
at the trainer's B so the targets align position-for-position.

* **retained prefix mass** — the statistic most likely to silently corrupt the
  target. **Already measured on real ruler text** (`RESULTS.md`, 2026-10-03):

  | byte depth | mean mass | p05 | fraction < 0.5 |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|
  | 0 | 0.803 | 0.079 | 13 % |
  | 1 | 0.371 | 0.0003 | 64 % |
  | 2 | 0.320 | 0.0000 | 69 % |

  Truncation is **not** the cause (full-vocab sweep barely moves it). The cause is
  prefix matching: the teacher puts ~63 % of its mass at depth ≥1 on tokens that
  **end** at that position, which MI discards. So the MI target at depth ≥1 is the
  teacher *conditioned on not ending the token here*. That is a **bias**, not
  noise, and it is the paper's stated approximation — it is what `<eot>` exists to
  recover. Report the statistic per arm; the backoff gate applies where mass
  falls under `floor` (default 0.05, now reported in `stats()`).

  **CORRECTION 2026-10-04:** these numbers were read as "the target is degenerate
  past depth 0". They are not — they are the retained mass of a *soft* target.
  With the corrected depth loop the target exists at every depth and has mean
  entropy 1.108 nats at depth 1. What the bias costs is *agreement with the gold
  byte* (top-1 == gold at only 44.9 % of depth-1 positions), which is a label-
  conflict measurement for the word-length-drift ruler, not a reason to drop the
  depths. See `RESULTS.md`, "The MI target was wrong at every depth >= 1".

### Step 4 — on-policy KD (later; needs a target bank)

Teacher labels **TMT's own rollouts**, replayed from a bank refreshed every
~2k steps (~5 % overhead, not 50 %). Must be **mixed** with real bytes: pure
on-policy amplifies existing modes (FS-N emitted 16 consecutive newlines).

## 5. Judge on two rulers, never CE alone

The paper's §6.6: at ~1.8e21 FLOPs all four byte models had *better* BPB and
*much worse* benchmark performance. An arm that wins held-out CE has not won.

**⚠ SUPERSEDED BY MEASUREMENT (2026-10-03):** the KD arm as specified cannot be
judged this way, because the Marginalize-It byte target is **one-hot at the gold
byte on 100.000000% of covered positions** (entropy exactly 0.000000 nats).
Conditioning on the gold prefix forces every surviving token to agree on byte
*d*, so `α·KL + (1−α)·CE` degenerates to position-dependent CE reweighting. The
arm is worse than the control from early on and diverges by step ~2,200. See
RESULTS.md, "Colab T4 smoke".

> **⚠ THIS ENTIRE BLOCK IS SUPERSEDED (2026-10-04) — read it as history, not as
> the operative target definition.** Both its claims are now known to be wrong, in
> opposite directions. (i) The one-hot result was real and came from an off-by-one
> in the depth loop, but (ii) the "fix" recorded here — emit depth 0 only and
> decline every depth >= 1 — was **false**, and the paper's own worked example
> contradicts it on the same page (depth 2: P(`isu`)=0.8 / P(`isk`)=0.2 from a
> single pass). The sentence "the only real signal available in one teacher pass
> is the unconditioned depth-0 first-byte marginal" is **withdrawn**. The operative
> definition is the paper's: condition on the bytes already emitted, aggregate the
> next one. Coverage 23.1% → **64.6%** (89.0% before the `floor`), mean target
> depth 1.26, mean entropy 0.536 nats. The corrected arm **does not diverge**
> (peak 7.29 at step 25, falling monotonically; the two closed arms peaked at
> 16.77 and 860.68). Nothing below in this section changes: the two-ruler gate,
> arm C as the control that must precede it, and word-length drift all stand. See
> `RESULTS.md`, "The MI target was wrong at every depth >= 1".

**Gate for every KD arm:** ruler CE ≥ control **and** no regression in the
6-seed degeneracy metrics (max newline run, unique bytes, entropy) **and** no
`User:`/`Assistant:` leakage. A CE-only win is reported as a *partial* result
with the disagreement stated, in the spirit of the 600k caveat in `RESULTS.md`.

**Third ruler, added after the fidelity measurement:** **word-length drift**.
The MI bias (gauge 2) predicts that a KD arm learns tokens that run longer than
they do. Measure mean bytes per generated word and the premature-line-end rate
against the CE control. One pass over the 250-byte samples; CE cannot see this.

## 7. NOT tested / known gaps

* One teacher, one seed (0), one τ (1). (The α=0.5 / no-sweep caveat is
  stated once, at the bottom of this list.)
* The "does a stronger teacher help or hurt?" arm (Llama-3.2-1B) is **out of
  scope by decision**, so we will not know whether teacher capacity matters.
* Near-paraphrase contamination of generated text is **not** detectable by the
  0.6 verbatim-line filter.
* Teacher quality is **no longer an assumption**: measured at
  0.6853 nats/byte on `tinystories_valid` (100 kB window) versus TMT's
  1.1741–1.1902, i.e. **~0.49 nats/byte better**. The distillation premise holds
  at 230 M. Ruler settings differ from the CE rows above; treat as magnitude,
  not verdict.
* MI is **not** the exact conversion the paper describes, at any storage budget:
  the loss is structural (tokens that end mid-token), not truncation. See gauge 2.
* One teacher, one seed (0), one α (0.5), one τ (1). No sweep — and see the
  prediction in `PLAN-2026-10-04-ROADMAP.md` P3: the objective is linear in the
  α/(1−α) mixture ratio and KD recovered only **half** of what its CE discount
  cost, so **α = 0 is predicted CE-optimal and a sweep is predicted to confirm a
  negative.** A sweep is worth running only if the word-length-drift ruler first
  shows a KD benefit CE cannot see.
* The KD term's effect on gradient SNR (the standing blocker) is **unmeasured**.
  If SNR does not move, distillation has not addressed the actual problem and
  the role-specific-LR factorial remains the next experiment.

  in parallel each get ~50 %, so wall clock does not improve.

**UPDATE 2026-10-03:** the KD term is nearly free (+1.5 % at B=256), and the
frozen-config rate is 1,060 B/s rather than 889. A 2,000-step B=1024 smoke is

shared dump is **~13 h**, not ~16 h.

**UPDATE (Colab, 2026-10-03):** KD is **+39.3 % on a T4** (39.8 vs 28.6 ms/step),
