## 1. The question

Do distilled **byte** models and distilled **token** models follow the *same*
scaling trends as compute and data grow? Small models are usually improved by
distillation from a bigger model and by overtraining; nobody had swept both
axes (tokenization × objective) at fixed size with a controlled comparison.

## 2. The mechanism they had to build first (idea 1)

To distil a token teacher into a byte student you must express the teacher's
next-token distribution over **bytes**. A token spans several bytes, so the
probability has to be split across byte positions. The known exact routes
(Hayase et al. 2025; Phan et al. 2024) need **one extra teacher forward pass per
byte position** — intractable for long sequences. They give two single-pass
conversions:

- **Marginalize-It (approximate).** Marginalize the teacher distribution over
  byte prefixes, conditioned on the ground-truth bytes already emitted.
  Exact for the first byte; for later bytes it *silently drops* competing
  tokenization paths (predicting `is`→`isu`/`isk` but forgetting that `is`
  itself could end there) and renormalizes the survivors. Fixing it exactly
  requires re-running the teacher on the alternative continuations.
- **End-Of-Token (exact).** Put an `<eot>` symbol in the vocabulary *and* in the
  sequence after every BPE token (~1 per 4.5 bytes). The residual probability has
  nowhere to leak — it lands on `<eot>` — so the teacher's distribution is
  preserved **exactly in one pass**.

**The finding inside the finding:** inserting `<eot>` costs **+30.94 % FLOPs per
unit of data** at fixed context, and that extra compute on a fixed amount of
data *by itself* improves the asymptote. So the exact method wins twice: by
preserving the distribution, and by buying compute per byte.

## 3. The experiment (idea 2: sweep both axes at fixed size)

Six arms = {`Tokens` (128,256) · `Bytes` (260) · `Bytes`+`<eot>` (261)} ×
{Cross-Entropy · distillation}, layer-parameter-matched at ~1.28 B (Token-1B is
1.81 B total because of the vocabulary). Llama-2 mixture; teacher Llama-3-8B; up
to 1 trillion bytes; 64×H200; AdamW; LR ∈ {1e-3, 4e-3, 8e-3}; global batch
~0.5 M units. Sequence length: 2048 tokens vs **9216 / 11264 byte units**, and
byte runs needed **qk-norm** to stop loss spikes. Eight benchmarks: ARC-Easy,
ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag, PIQA (MCQA); MBPP, Natural Questions (generation);
Flores en↔de (translation, BLEU).

## 4. What they found

### 4a. Byte models start worse and end higher

| arm | asympt. BPB | asympt. avg. downstream acc. |
|---|---:|---:|
| Token Supervised | 0.9568 | 46.0 |
| Token Distilled | 0.9407 | 48.4 |
| Bytes Supervised | **0.8967** | 51.2 |
| Marginalize-It Distilled | 0.9016 | 50.5 |
| Bytes + `<eot>` Supervised | **0.8891** | 51.6 |
| **End-Of-Token Distilled** | 0.8983 | **52.4** |

Token models lead at low FLOP and **plateau**; byte models climb faster and
reach a higher ceiling. Distillation beats CE at matched compute for tokens — but
for plain `Bytes` the *approximation* costs more than distillation gains
(50.5 < 51.2). **Exactness is what makes distillation win for bytes.**

### 4b. Lower BPB did **not** mean a better model (idea 3, the most important one)

At ~1.8e21 FLOPs all four byte models had **better** validation BPB than
Token-Supervised and were **much worse on every benchmark**. They fit
error-vs-loss per arm, `z(y) = ε − k·e^{−γy}`, and got six different curves.
Conclusion (§6.6, App. G): **BPB is not a fair comparison across tokenizations or
objectives.** BPB is a function of `p_correct` alone — it is blind to how the
remaining `1 − p_correct` mass is distributed; downstream accuracy depends on
whether the target is the **argmax**, i.e. on the residual mass BPB ignores. Two
models can share an identical BPB and decode completely different text.

### 4c. Iso-FLOP "feather plots" give the crossover (idea 4)

Sample FLOPs, map through the BPB law, then through the error law, and connect
iso-FLOP points across arms. Crossover to Token-Distilled's asymptote:

| method | compute needed | unique text needed |
|---|---:|---:|
| Marginalize-It Distilled | 2.04e23 FLOPs | 1.12e14 byte-equiv |
| **End-Of-Token Distilled** | **6.33e22 FLOPs** | **3.48e13 byte-equiv** |

→ **~3.2× less compute, ~6.35× ("one-sixth") less unique text** (the text number
corrects for the inserted `<eot>` symbols, of which only 4.5/5.5 are content).

### 4d. Storage: the byte student needs no top-k (idea 5)

Offline distillation is bottlenecked by teacher-logit storage (2T tokens ×
128,256 floats ≈ 1.03 **exabytes**), which is why top-*k* (~600) truncation is
the norm. A byte student needs the teacher's **full** 260-value distribution per
byte — no truncation at all, ~**1/5** the footprint.

### 4e. Open-weight comparison (extrapolated, not measured)

Predicted asymptote beats Llama-3.2-1B (45.9), Gemma-3-1B-pt (44.3), Gemma 2B
(50.3) on averaged downstream tasks, by up to 6.5 % / 8.1 % / 2.1 %.

## 5. Limits the paper states itself

Dense transformers only; ~fixed size; some large runs **epoch**; 8 benchmarks;
byte models remain **expensive at inference** and are *not* matched on inference
cost; MoE untested; "understanding the training dynamics of distilling large byte
transformers into smaller ones remains an open question." The headline 4–6 %
asymptote gaps are **extrapolations** of power-law fits (R² ≈ 0.998–1.000) far
outside the measured compute range, with the headline LR fixed at 4e-3.

## 6. What this means for TMT — hypotheses, each with a named test

**The mechanism is already ours; the thesis is now externally supported.**

1. **TMT is permanently in the `End-Of-Token` regime.** 256-byte vocabulary, no
   tokenizer, no BPE-boundary problem, no top-*k* truncation — ideas 1 and 5
   (the paper's engineering contributions) do not apply to TMT because TMT never
   had the problem they solve. Nothing to implement.
2. **TMT is far left of the crossover.** The paper's own crossover sits at
   ~1e22 FLOPs; TMT has 4.5 M params and has seen ~0.9 GB. So the current
   incoherent samples are the *predicted* state of a byte model at this budget,
   **not evidence against the architecture**. Test: keep judging the architecture
   by the *slope* of the CE-vs-bytes curve and by memory formation (hot dims,
   half-life), not by absolute CE at 10⁵ steps.
3. **Distillation becomes cheap for TMT.** Because the student's vocabulary is
   260 values, a teacher dump is 260 floats/byte with no truncation. Two arms the
   repo has never run: (a) **self-distillation** — teacher = an earlier/larger TMT
   checkpoint, student = the current config; (b) **token-teacher distillation** via
   Marginalize-It/EoT. Test: does the student beat its CE-supervised twin at
   matched bytes, steps and ruler setting (the 0.03 CE bar)?
4. **Insert a boundary symbol in the byte stream.** The paper's cheapest positive
   lever is +30.9 % compute/unit from one inserted symbol per ~4.5 bytes. TMT's
   stop head *predicts* line ends but never *consumes* one — it is a target,
   not an input symbol. Test: a from-scratch arm trained on streams with an
   explicit boundary byte, byte-matched against an identical arm without it, judged
   on held-out CE **and** the fixed-prompt generation probe.
5. **Ruler upgrade — adopt §6.6 as a rule.** Never compare CE across incomparable
   arms (different objective, tokenization, corpus or architecture); fit the
   per-arm error-vs-loss curve and compare at **iso-loss**, then confirm with a
   fixed-prompt generation probe and a multi-domain eval. The repo has already
   been bitten twice: FS-W won CE and lost novels by 0.96, and the 600k
   checkpoint's in-domain CE trend "does not describe generation quality"
   (`../../../RESULTS.md`).
6. **Data quantity is not TMT's lever.** The paper's 6.35× data-efficiency claim
   is about matching a token baseline; TMT has no compute budget to trade data
   against, and it already measured that **corpus identity** dominates at matched
   bytes (0.55 CE between TinyStories and Gutenberg). So the paper's data result
   is *permission* to stay byte-level, not a reason to change the corpus.

## 7. What NOT to read into it

Every conclusion is conditional on a dense ~1.28 B transformer with attention, a
cosine schedule, a Llama-3-8B teacher and 64 H200s. It says **nothing** about
TMT's O(1)-memory claim, the JEPA latent objective, or the recurrent trace units,
in either direction. And by this repo's own standard — "a test that passes is a
hypothesis, not a proof" — their asymptotes are fitted-then-extrapolated curves,
which is exactly the kind of claim `RESULTS.md` refuses to make without a ruler
setting attached.
