# Paper notes — "Don't Decay the Learning Rate, Increase the Batch Size" (arXiv:1711.00489v2)

**Paper:** Samuel L. Smith, Pieter-Jan Kindermans, Chris Ying, Quoc V. Le — *Don't
Decay the Learning Rate, Increase the Batch Size*, arXiv:1711.00489v2 [cs.LG,
cs.CV, cs.DC, stat.ML], ICLR 2018.

**Source text:** `1711.00489v2.paper.txt`; `1711.00489v2.paper.html.gz`.

**Verification status: VERIFIED, with one caveat on scope.** Abstract page fetched
2026-10-05; title and authors match. The attributed claim — *"increasing batch can
substitute for an LR schedule, tested with Adam as well as SGD"* — is **literally
in the abstract** ("This procedure is successful for stochastic gradient descent
(SGD), SGD with momentum, Nesterov momentum, and Adam"). The caveat: the Adam
experiment is one panel (Fig. 4b, Wide ResNet on CIFAR-10, TensorFlow defaults
`lr=1e-3`, `β₁=0.9`, `β₂=0.999`). The paper's headline results (ResNet-50 /
ImageNet 76.1 % in 30 min; Inception-ResNet-V2 77 % in 2500 updates) are **SGD
based**. The Adam evidence is a demonstration that the substitution holds, not a
scale-validated result.

**Status of this note:** external ideas, not a plan and not a result.

---

## 1. What they actually show

Three separate "repurpose a decay schedule as batch growth" rules:

| rule | relation | paper's own caveat |
|---|---|---|
| increase `B` instead of decaying `ε` | `B` absorbs the decay | the primary result |
| raise `ε` | `B ∝ ε` | — |
| raise momentum `m` | `B ∝ 1/(1−m)` | "tends to slightly reduce the test accuracy" |

They state the operational claim precisely: **the same training and test curves**,
reached with **fewer parameter updates**, and — the part that matters for a
research repo — **with no hyper-parameter tuning**, because an existing published
schedule is converted arithmetically.

## 2. What this means for TMT

**This is the closest published precedent for TMT's blocked lever, and TMT's
blocking claim is precisely the one this paper removes.** `RESULTS.md` records that
the global `--lr` has **never been swept**. The external recommendation was framed
as "an accumulation experiment." This paper says the accumulation size and the
learning rate are the *same knob*, with a known conversion, and — critically — that
converting an existing schedule needs no new tuning. TMT therefore does not need an
LR sweep *and* an accumulation sweep; it needs a 1-D sweep over `B ∝ ε` with `B=1`
as the control.

Two TMT-specific consequences:

1. **It makes the SNR number interpretable.** If TMT's SNR ≈ 0.05 is an
   over-large-step artefact (as `RESULTS.md` concludes from the frozen-θ 0.633
   reading), then scaling `B ∝ ε` at fixed epochs should move SNR toward the frozen
   value. That is a *prediction with a direction*, which the repo can falsify.
2. **The "fewer parameter updates" axis is TMT's real budget axis.** TMT has
   ~890 k steps and no GPU-scale parallelism to buy. Smith et al.'s benefit here is
   wall-clock parallelism, which TMT cannot use. The transferable half is only the
   equivalence of "schedule the LR" and "schedule the batch."

## 3. What this paper does NOT support

- It does **not** claim that batch growth is *always* a substitute. "It is common
  practice to decay the learning rate. Here we show one can **usually** obtain the
  same learning curve…"
- It does **not** say anything about Adam's second moment, `β₂`, SNR, noise-dominated
  denominators, or adaptive-method failure modes. The Adam panel simply inherits the
  behaviour; nothing is explained.
- It does **not** test recurrent networks, byte-level models, or any
  architecture-specific claim. All architectures are CNNs.
- It does **not** transfer its *hyper-parameters*: the rule is about converting a
  schedule, not about which schedule to pick. Any TMT-specific value would have to
  come from TMT's own sweep.
- The momentum rule is explicitly not free — it slightly hurts test accuracy.

## 4. Bearing on the three open questions

- **Noise floor vs over-large step?** Strong support for **over-large step**, and
  it gives the scaling law that turns the question into an experiment: hold epochs
  fixed, set `B ∝ ε`, and watch whether SNR moves toward 0.633.
- **Estimator vs optimiser?** This paper's answer is *neither alone* — it is the
  **interaction**, and it is the first entry on the shelf that says the optimiser's
  LR schedule and the estimator's batch size are interchangeable. So an accumulation
  sweep is genuinely an optimiser sweep in disguise.
- **Input-dependent gating?** No bearing.
