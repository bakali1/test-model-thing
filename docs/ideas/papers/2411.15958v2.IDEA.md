# Paper notes — "Adaptive Methods through the Lens of SDEs" (arXiv:2411.15958v2)

**Paper:** Enea Monzio Compagnoni, Tianlin Liu, Rustem Islamov, Frank Norbert Proske,
Antonio Orvieto, Aurelien Lucchi — *Adaptive Methods through the Lens of SDEs:
Theoretical Insights on the Role of Noise*, arXiv:2411.15958v2 [cs.LG],
**accepted at ICLR 2025 (Poster)** (comments field: "An earlier version, titled
'SDEs for Adaptive Methods: The Role of Noise' and dated May 2024, is available on
OpenReview").

**Source text:** `2411.15958v2.paper.txt`; `2411.15958v2.paper.html.gz`.

**Verification status: VERIFIED, and the venue claim is correct** (ICLR 2025). The
attributed claim needs one correction — see §2.

---

## 1. What they actually show

They derive SDEs (order-1 weak approximations) for **SignSGD**, **RMSprop(W)** and
**Adam(W)**, and validate them by numerically integrating with Euler–Maruyama on
MLPs, CNNs, ResNets and Transformers ("our SDEs accurately track the behavior of the
respective optimizers"). They are explicit that they work under **weaker
assumptions** than prior Adam/RMSprop SDEs, and say "Adam and RMSprop are
byproducts of our analysis."

Verified results:

- **SignSGD** has three phases, governed by the **signal-to-noise ratio**
  `Σ^{-1/2}∇f(x)`. In phase 3 the *noise itself* premultiplies the signal, so noise
  **inversely** affects the convergence rate — unlike SGD, where `Σ` appears only
  in the diffusion term and does not affect the rate.
- Asymptotic loss: **linear in `σ_max` for SignSGD, quadratic for SGD.** This is the
  central SignSGD-vs-SGD contrast.
- SignSGD is **resilient to infinite-variance noise** (Student-`t`): the drift
  constants for Gaussian vs infinite-variance noise are only ~10 % apart, and the
  diffusion coefficients are comparable. "In the same conditions, SGD diverges."
- **For vanilla Adam and RMSprop**, the effect of noise at convergence **mimics
  SignSGD** (asymptotic loss and covariance scale linearly in `Σ^{1/2}`, Fig. 6).
- **For AdamW and RMSpropW it is different:** decoupled weight decay stabilises at
  high noise near the minimiser, so the asymptotic loss is *bounded* in `σ` rather
  than linear.

## 2. The claim that needed correcting

The external gloss said this paper shows "**Adam becomes indistinguishable from
SignSGD when the second moment is noise-dominated**."

**Partly supported, and the phrasing overstates it in one direction and misses the
important one.** What the paper actually says (its own §1 conclusion):

> "while for **vanilla Adam and RMSprop** the effect of noise at convergence
> mimics SignSGD, something different happens in **AdamW and RMSpropW** — due to an
> intricate interaction between noise, curvature, and regularization, decoupled
> weight decay plays a crucial stabilization role at high noise levels near the
> minimizer."

So: (a) the statement is about the **asymptotic loss and covariance scaling** on
**convex quadratics**, not about the update becoming `sign(m)`; (b) it applies to
**vanilla Adam/RMSprop**, and **explicitly does not apply to AdamW** — which is what
TMT actually runs. Do not cite this paper as "AdamW degrades to SignSGD"; it says
the opposite. (c) The abstract's own summary is more cautious: "for AdamW and
RMSpropW, the role of noise is **much more complex**."

That correction matters here specifically because `RESULTS.md` records
**"AdamW ≡ SGD here"** for TMT (normalized SGD with a constant normalizer,
`|update|/|m|` spread 1.0020×). TMT's AdamW is not showing weight-decay
stabilisation in the sense this paper describes, because its denominator is
effectively constant.

## 3. What this means for TMT

The one transferable piece is **the SignSGD prediction, and it is a prediction TMT
can falsify.** This paper's SignSGD SDE says the asymptotic loss scales **linearly
in `σ`**, and that even infinite-variance noise barely degrades SignSGD while SGD
diverges. If TMT's optimiser is SGD-with-a-constant-normalizer (the repo's own
finding), then TMT sits in the regime where **this paper says SGD is the fragile
one** and SignSGD would be the robust one. That is a genuinely surprising,
testable, and cheap prediction: an untested arm.

Named test: a SignSGD / Lion-style constant-magnitude arm (`update = −lr·sign(m)`,
which the repo has evidently implemented before, since it *retracted* the claim
"Adam degenerates to `sign(m)`" — the arm exists) versus the current normalized
arm, at matched wall-clock, judged on held-out CE and the fixed-prompt generation
probe. Careful: the retraction exists precisely because equating the two was
previously wrong, so this must be run as an *arm*, not asserted as an identity.

The SNR-relevant reading: the paper's insistence that **adaptivity, gradient noise
and curvature interact** means "measure SNR" and "measure the curvature" are not
separable diagnostics. TMT's 0.05-vs-0.633 gap is a statement about all three.

## 4. What this paper does NOT support

- **Not an empirical study of the optimizers it models.** The validation is that the
  *SDEs* track the optimizers. No claim is made (or tested) that following any
  scaling rule derived from the SDE improves a real network.
- **The theory is on convex quadratics and (assumed-smooth) objectives.** The
  SignSGD three-phase analysis is stated for `∇f_γ(x) = ∇f(x) + Z`, `Z ~ N(0,Σ)`,
  `Σ = diag(σ²ᵢ)`, i.e. **isotropic-per-coordinate Gaussian** noise. TMT's gradient
  noise is neither Gaussian nor stationary.
- **It does not transfer to recurrent or byte-level models.** The architecture
  validation is MLPs/CNNs/ResNets/Transformers on vision tasks.
- **It says nothing about second-moment discriminative power**, i.e. nothing about
  TMT's measured non-discriminating `v_t`.
- **It says nothing about memory horizons, decay parameters, or the 8,192-of-
  4,481,793 `.decay` slice.**
- **It does not address β₂ tuning**, or why adaptive learning rates become
  "unstable or extreme" late in training (that is MaxVA's and Reddi et al.'s
  subject; see [`2006.11918v4.IDEA.md`](2006.11918v4.IDEA.md) and
  [`1904.09237v1.IDEA.md`](1904.09237v1.IDEA.md)).
- Its own §2 notes that the heavy-tail/class-imbalance angle is prior work by
  *others*; do not re-attribute it here.

## 5. Bearing on the three open questions

- **Noise floor vs over-large step?** Supports the **over-large-step / noise-
  dominated** reading in a specific way: the paper's regime is one where the noise
  scale `√Σ` *itself* sets the descent rate. That is exactly the "large effective
  temperature" picture TMT's SNR number describes.
- **Estimator vs optimiser?** Decisively **optimiser** — SignSGD vs SGD under
  identical noise.
- **Input-dependent gating?** No bearing.
