## 1. What the paper actually claims

It is a **pure theory** paper. STORM (see [`1905.10018v3.IDEA.md`](1905.10018v3.IDEA.md))
had guarantees that required **average smoothness** (mean-square smoothness:
`E‖g(x,ξ) − g(y,ξ)‖² ≤ L²‖x−y‖²`). This paper:

- re-derives the average-smoothness results (`O(T^{-1/3})` nonconvex;
  `O(σ²/(μT))` last-iterate under μ-PL);
- then drops average smoothness and proves, under **standard** smoothness alone,
  `O(T^{-1/4})` for nonconvex — "which matches the classical SGD rate and is optimal
  under standard assumptions" — via an auxiliary sequence and stepsize blocks;
- and gives optimal convex / `λ`-strongly-convex rates, `O(σR/√T)` averaged and
  `O(σ²/(λT))` last-iterate.

Two details matter for reading the title: **"geometries" here means *smoothness
geometries* (standard vs average vs PL), not Riemannian geometry and not
parameter-space geometry** — despite the field tags `math.OC`. And **all results
use the same STORM recursion with different hyper-parameter choices** (the
harmonic schedule, plus "stepsize blocks").

## 2. What this means for TMT

Almost nothing directly, and the reason is worth stating so this note is not
misused. TMT's `RESULTS.md` diagnosis is that the SNR ≈ 0.05 blocker is an
**over-large-step artefact** (same estimator, frozen θ → 0.633). This paper's entire
contribution is about which *smoothness assumption* is needed to prove a rate. It
does not change a learning rate, a batch size, or a denominator.

The one honest use: **it is the strongest available statement that STORM's benefit
does not depend on a smoothness assumption TMT has certainly violated.** Since
TMT's loss on a byte stream is not plausibly average-smooth, the `O(T^{-1/3})`
guarantee that motivates STORM does not formally apply, and `O(T^{-1/4})` — the SGD
rate — is the honest ceiling. In other words: **if TMT adopts STORM, the theory
gives it no advantage over SGD.** Cite this when scoping expectations, not as a
reason to adopt it.

Secondary use: the μ-PL result (`O(σ²/(μT))`) is the first linear-rate statement on
this shelf. If a TMT arm ever satisfies PL locally, this is the reference rate to
compare against. TMT has not measured that.

## 3. What this paper does NOT support

- **No experiments.** It is theory only. Nothing here was measured on any network.
- **No language models, no recurrent nets, no byte-level models.** Only convex,
  strongly convex, and nonconvex smooth objectives with a stochastic oracle.
- **It does not strengthen STORM's practical case** — the rate drops from
  `O(T^{-1/3})` to `O(T^{-1/4})` once average smoothness is dropped, and
  `O(T^{-1/4})` is *exactly SGD's rate*. The paper is a robustness result, not an
  improvement.
- **It does not address Adam, adaptive denominators, SNR, or β₂.**
- **It does not address the parameter-space geometry of an RNN.** The word
  "geometries" in the title is about the smoothness condition; do not cite it as
  evidence that STORM is robust on ill-conditioned or low-rank parameter spaces.
  (On that, the more relevant item is [`2206.11893v2.IDEA.md`](2206.11893v2.IDEA.md):
  diagonal SSMs are "surprisingly sensitive to low-rank perturbations.")
- **Recency caveat.** Four days old, no citations, no peer review. Treat it as a
  preprint that happens to check out, not as an established result. If it is
  superseded, this note is wrong and nothing else in the shelf depends on it.
