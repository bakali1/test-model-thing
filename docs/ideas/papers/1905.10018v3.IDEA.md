# Paper notes — "Momentum-Based Variance Reduction in Non-Convex SGD" / STORM (arXiv:1905.10018v3)

**Paper:** Ashok Cutkosky, Francesco Orabona — *Momentum-Based Variance Reduction in
Non-Convex SGD*, arXiv:1905.10018v3 [cs.LG, math.OC, stat.ML].

**Source text:** `1905.10018v3.paper.txt`; `1905.10018v3.paper.html.gz`.

**Verification status: VERIFIED.** Abstract page fetched 2026-10-05; title and
authors match, and the algorithm is named **STORM** in the body. The attributed
claim — *"the interesting answer to 'can we suppress stochastic gradient variance
without huge batches?'"* — is verified against §1 and §7: STORM "does not require
any batches and makes use of adaptive learning rates… its technique for removing
the batches uses a variant of momentum to achieve variance reduction," giving
`E[‖∇F(x)‖] ≤ O(1/√T + σ^{1/3}/T^{1/3})` **without knowledge of σ**.

**Status of this note:** external ideas, not a plan and not a result.

---

## 1. The mechanism, stated precisely

STORM keeps a running estimate `y_t` of the gradient and updates

`x_{t+1} = x_t − η_t ∇f(x_t, ξ_t) + η_t ∇f(y_t, ξ'_t)`,

i.e. it adds back a **gradient evaluated at a smoothed/stale point** rather than at
`x_t`. The variance reduction comes from the difference of two noisy gradients at
*different* points — which is why it needs no mega-batch and no checkpoint
schedule. `η_t` is chosen adaptively from the observed noise level, so the
algorithm "enjoys the same robustness to learning rate tuning as popular algorithms
like AdaGrad or Adam" (§7).

The authors are explicit that variance reduction has **not** delivered in practice,
and name the two reasons STORM removes: non-adaptive learning rates, and giant
batches to build the low-noise checkpoint gradient.

## 2. What this means for TMT

TMT's stated blocker is a **gradient SNR of ≈ 0.05** in training (0.633 frozen),
and the memory horizon is 8 bytes — the model retains roughly 8 bytes of state
across a byte stream. STORM is the one paper on this shelf whose subject is
precisely "variance reduction at the batch size you already have."

Two concrete transfers:

1. **STORM's `y_t` is a second evaluation point, i.e. an extra forward pass.** TMT's
   cost model is bytes × params; one extra forward per step is a real but not fatal
   cost for a 4.48 M-parameter model. The comparison that matters is
   *SNR per FLOP*, not SNR per step.
2. **STORM's stale point is a form of memory, and TMT has a memory wall.** If TMT's
   8-byte horizon is the reason gradients are unpredictable, then a method that
   reads the gradient at a *slightly different* iterate is probing whether
   unpredictability is a property of the point or of the byte. This gives the memory
   question an optimisation-side experiment that does not require touching the
   architecture.

Named test: a STORM arm at the current `--lr` and at the Smith et al. `B ∝ ε`
setting, judged on held-out CE and the fixed-prompt generation probe. Log SNR per
step for STORM and for the control; if STORM's SNR rises materially at equal CE, the
noise was estimator variance and not an architectural deficit.

## 3. What this paper does NOT support

- It does **not** show STORM helps deep networks. The experiments are **CIFAR-10 +
  ResNet** only, and §7 says only that STORM "indeed seems to be optimizing the
  objective in fewer iterations than baseline algorithms." "Seems" is theirs.
- It does **not** apply to recurrent or autoregressive models. The theory assumes
  `f(x, ξ)` is `L`-smooth in `x`; an RNN unrolled through a byte stream is smooth
  in `x` but the noise model (`Z(x)`) and the Lipschitz constant both change with
  every byte of the stream.
- It does **not** give a per-coordinate or per-module result. STORM's variance
  reduction is a *scalar* guarantee on `E[‖∇F‖]`. It says nothing about TMT's 8,192
  dedicated decay coordinates (0.183 % of 4,481,793 params) getting a
  differently-scaled step.
- It does **not** establish anything about Adam's second moment or about whether an
  adaptive denominator is the right object. Its adaptive `η_t` is a scalar rule.
- The theory needs `f` `L`-smooth and `L`-Lipschitz-in-`ξ`; TMT has neither
  measured. Do not import the `O(1/√T + σ^{1/3}/T^{1/3})` rate as a prediction.

## 4. Bearing on the three open questions

- **Noise floor vs over-large step?** Directly relevant and **ambiguous by
  construction**: STORM's premise is that variance can be reduced at fixed batch,
  i.e. the noise is *estimator* variance, not an intrinsic floor. A positive STORM
  result is evidence for the estimator reading; a null result at equal compute is
  evidence for the floor reading. This is a clean two-sided test.
- **Estimator vs optimiser?** STORM is the *estimator* arm of this shelf (it changes
  what gradient is computed), and it is the only one that does.
- **Input-dependent gating?** No bearing — STORM's improvement is uniform across
  coordinates, which is mild evidence against needing architectural gating.
