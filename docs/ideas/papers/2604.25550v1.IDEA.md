## 1. Claims that ARE in the paper

**Claim 1 — SNR-weighted stationarity measure under symmetric noise. VERIFIED.**
§III defines, per coordinate, `s_{k,i} = √Var(g̃_{k,i}|x_k)` and the per-coordinate
SNR `S_{k,i} = |g_{k,i}|/s_{k,i}`, then

`Φ_k := Σ_i |g_{k,i}|·min(1, S_{k,i}) = Σ_i min(|g_{k,i}|, |g_{k,i}|²/s_{k,i})`.

Their words: "`Φ_k` smoothly interpolates between `‖g_k‖₁` on high-SNR coordinates and
a quadratic-in-`|g_{k,i}|` measure on low-SNR coordinates, **reflecting the
diminishing usefulness of the sign bit when noise dominates the signal**." The
assumption required is that per-coordinate noise is **unimodal and symmetric**, which
is what makes the Gauss-inequality sign-failure bound apply coordinate-wise.
Theorem 1: with constant batch `n` and stepsize `1/√(L̃₁K)`,
`E[(1/K)ΣΦ_k] ≤ 3√L̃₁/√K (f₀−f*+½)`, and separately
`E[(1/K)Σ‖g_k‖₁] ≤ 3√L̃₁/√K (f₀−f*+½) + σ̃₁/√n`.

**Claim 2 — annealed Gaussian "dithering" injected before the sign operator.
VERIFIED.** §IV / Algorithm 1: pre-sign noise `ξ_i ~ N(0, σ_k²)` with
`σ_k² = α(1+k)^{-γ}`, applied to SignSGD **with momentum**. Their mechanism argument:
with momentum signal `m_i > 0`, the probability of correct sign is `Φ(m_i/σ_k)`, and
for `|m_i| ≲ σ_k` the expected update is `≈ m_i√(2/π)/σ_k` — i.e. **proportional to
the magnitude, not to its sign**, "partially restoring the magnitude information lost
to quantization." The annealing schedule makes this strongest early and decays it.
The empirical claim: post-sign injection gives "at most a marginal benefit" and
"destabilizes training at higher noise levels"; pre-sign injection "surpasses Adam
on CIFAR-100."

## 2. The claim that is NOT in the paper — REFUSED

**Claim 3 — "and a critical batch size `B_noise`. NOT SUPPORTED. DO NOT CITE.**

Verified by exhaustive search of the full text of the paper: the strings
`B_noise`, `Bnoise`, "critical batch size", and "critical batch" **do not occur**,
and there is no quantity in the paper that plays that role.

What the paper actually has is the **opposite kind of statement** — a *non-vanishing
noise floor* whose value is `σ̃₁/√n`, i.e. it **decreases** as batch grows and is
**never thresholded**:

> "Theorem 1 holds for any constant `n`, at the price of a **non-vanishing
> `σ̃₁/√n` floor** … This floor is an honest reflection of the cost of 1-bit
> quantization at small batch sizes: when the per-coordinate SNR is low, the sign bit
> carries little information about the gradient direction, and no amount of
> iteration can drive `‖g_k‖₁` below the noise level encoded in `σ̃₁/√n`."

There is no "critical batch size" defined, derived, or discussed anywhere. The
attribution is almost certainly a **conflation with McCandlish et al.
1812.06162**, which *does* define `B_noise` (the gradient noise scale) — see
[`1812.06162v1.IDEA.md`](1812.06162v1.IDEA.md). The two papers have nothing to do
with each other beyond both mentioning noise and batching.

## 3. What this means for TMT

- **`Φ_k` is a ready-made refinement of TMT's SNR ruler.** TMT measures a single
  scalar SNR (≈ 0.05 moving, 0.633 frozen) on a 4,481,793-parameter byte model. `Φ_k`
  is a *per-coordinate, SNR-weighted* stationarity measure that distinguishes "the
  gradient is small because we are near a minimum" from "the gradient is small
  because the noise swamps it." Under TMT's numbers those are exactly the two
  hypotheses the repo is trying to separate, and the *plain* SNR conflates them.
  Named test: compute `Φ_k` alongside the repo's SNR estimator for ≥100 steps at a
  frozen θ and while training. If `Φ_k` falls toward 0 while the plain SNR stays at
  0.05, the gradients are *informative* and the step is too large (the repo's
  current diagnosis is confirmed). If `Φ_k` stays flat, the sign bit / per-coordinate
  signal really is noise and the floor reading gains support.
- **Pre-sign dithering is a cheap, architecture-free probe of the same question.**
  If adding annealed Gaussian noise before a quantiser makes the expected update
  proportional to `m` rather than `sign(m)`, that is a direct test of whether TMT's
  update is magnitude-starved. But TMT does **not** use a sign operator — it uses
  Adam's `m/√v` — so this is only transferable as a *diagnostic*, not as a fix.
- **Their reported effect is dataset-dependent and modest.** Plain SignSGD-M trails
  tuned SGD by ~0.6 % on CIFAR-10 but ~12 % on CIFAR-100. The calibrated switch
  helps on CIFAR-10 (92.18 % vs SGD 91.38 %) and **fails to match SGD on
  CIFAR-100**. Read that as: a harder task separates the methods, and the authors
  could not close the gap on the harder one.

## 4. What this paper does NOT support

- **No critical batch size.** See §2. This is the load-bearing correction.
- **No claim about Adam.** "Pre-sign dithering surpasses Adam on CIFAR-100" is a
  ResNet-18 single-worker number on one dataset; it is not a statement about Adam's
  `v_t` or `β₂`.
- **Single-worker ResNet-18 only.** No multi-worker majority-vote aggregation (the
  authors list it as future work), no RNNs, no byte-level models, no LMs.
- **The dithering theory is borrowed from 1-bit A/D conversion** (Schuchman 1964),
  and their own contribution is the connection plus a schedule. Do not cite it as a
  general result about adding noise to gradients.
- **"Improves test accuracy" ≠ "converges better."** `Φ_k` is a *stationarity
  measure*, not a loss bound; a vanishing `Φ_k` is a weaker statement than a
  vanishing loss.
- Five pages, three figures, one seed's worth of experiments as far as the text
  shows; treat every number as preliminary.
- Nothing about memory horizons, decay parameters (TMT's 8,192 of 4,481,793), or
  the architecture.

## 5. Bearing on the three open questions

- **Noise floor vs over-large step?** This is the only paper on the shelf that
  provides an instrument capable of *answering* that question rather than assuming
  an answer — see the `Φ_k` test in §3.
- **Estimator vs optimiser?** Optimiser (SignSGD-M, SWATS-style switching).
- **Input-dependent gating?** No bearing.
