## 1. What they actually prove

1. **An explicit counterexample**: a simple convex problem on `[-1,1]` where
   **Adam does not converge to the optimum.** With `β₁=0.9, β₂=0.99` (the usual
   practice), Adam's iterates converge to `x=1` — the point of *largest* regret —
   while its average regret `R_t/t` does not go to 0. AMSGrad's regret converges to
   0 and its iterates reach the optimum (Fig. 1, both online and stochastic).
2. **The cause is the second-moment EMA itself.** The failure traces to `Γ_t` (the
   quantity Adam divides by) not being positive semidefinite, which invalidates
   Kingma & Ba's convergence proof — a specific, identifiable error, plus problems
   with their Lemmas 10.3 and 10.4.
3. **The fix is "long-term memory"**: the running **maximum** of `v_t` rather than
   the EMA, giving a non-increasing step size (`Γ_t ⪰ 0` for all `t`). They note
   Adagrad's accumulator has the opposite pathology — it *decreases* the step too
   much over long periods, "which often leads to poor performance in practice."
4. Empirical: AMSGrad beats Adam on logistic regression/MNIST and CIFAR-10 Cifarnet,
   train and test, and is "relatively more robust to parameter changes."

## 2. What this means for TMT

This paper names the exact hyperparameter the TMT optimiser report is about.
`RESULTS.md`: Adam's second moment in TMT is **non-discriminating** — the denominator
is effectively constant (`|update|/|m|` spread 1.0020×), i.e. **AdamW ≡ SGD here**
with a global normalizer. Reddi et al. say two things about that state:

- **A near-constant `Γ_t` is a β₂-decay problem, and AMSGrad is the principled
  repair.** Reddi's counterexample uses `β₂ = 0.99`; TMT's AdamW uses
  `β₂ = 0.999` — a *slower* decay, i.e. even more memory of the largest gradient seen
  and a denominator even more dominated by a few old coordinates. If TMT's
  denominator is constant because it is dominated by a few stale large gradients,
  then AMSGrad's running-max would make it *more* constant, not less. The
  informative experiment is therefore the opposite direction: **shorten `β₂`** (or
  reset the accumulator), which is what Adafactor's "gradually increasing decay rate
  scheme" does and what MaxVA ([`2006.11918v4.IDEA.md`](2006.11918v4.IDEA.md))
  formalises.
- **The concrete second-moment experiment TMT has not run.** Log the per-coordinate
  `v_t` distribution and check whether it is flat because all `g²` are equal, or
  because a few coordinates dominate the EMA. Those two cases call for opposite
  fixes and nothing in the current report distinguishes them.

Named test: sweep `β₂ ∈ {0.9, 0.99, 0.999, 0.9999}` at fixed `--lr` and fixed
`B`, logging the spread of `v_t` and the per-coordinate SNR correlation; judge on
held-out CE + fixed-prompt generation probe. This is a **one-line config change** and
is the cheapest untested lever on the entire shelf.

## 3. What this paper does NOT support

- **It does not say AMSGrad is better in practice.** The paper's own contribution is
  a *convergence guarantee plus a non-convergence counterexample*; the empirical
  section is MNIST logistic regression and CIFAR-10 Cifarnet. AMSGrad's practical
  reputation is mixed and it is not the default anywhere.
- **The counterexample is a synthetic 1-D convex problem.** It does not establish
  that any deep network fails to train with Adam. Do not cite it as "Adam doesn't
  work on transformers."
- **It says nothing about noise, SNR, noise scale, or gradient noise temperature.**
  The mechanism is entirely about the shape of `Γ_t`, not about how noisy `g` is.
  TMT's SNR ≈ 0.05 problem is not this paper's problem.
- **It does not cover AdamW / decoupled weight decay**, nor any recurrent, byte-level,
  or long-horizon model. TMT runs AdamW, which this analysis does not address — and
  ICLR-2025's SDE analysis ([`2411.15958v2.IDEA.md`](2411.15958v2.IDEA.md)) shows
  decoupled weight decay *changes* the high-noise stability picture entirely.
- **The step-size argument needs `α_t = α/√t`** (and, for the theorem, a decreasing
  `β₁` schedule and `γ = β₁/√β₂`). TMT almost certainly uses constant `α` and
  constant `β₁`, so the *guarantee* does not apply to TMT's setting even though the
  *diagnosis* may. Do not quote the `O(σ√(G²T)/T)` regret bound as a TMT prediction.
- The proposed variants' "often also lead to improved empirical performance" is
  empirical and modest.

## 4. Bearing on the three open questions

- **Noise floor vs over-large step?** Indirect but real: this paper shows the
  denominator can be pathological *independently of* any noise measurement, so a low
  SNR reading is not by itself evidence of a noise floor. It is a third
  explanation: the second-moment estimator is mis-shaped.
- **Estimator vs optimiser?** Decisively **optimiser**, and the cheapest intervention
  on the shelf (one `β₂` sweep).
- **Input-dependent gating?** No bearing.
