# Paper notes — "MaxVA: Fast Adaptation of Step Sizes by Maximizing Observed Variance of Gradients" (arXiv:2006.11918v4)

**Paper:** Chen Zhu, Yu Cheng, Zhe Gan, Furong Huang, Jingjing Liu, Tom Goldstein —
*MaxVA: Fast Adaptation of Step Sizes by Maximizing Observed Variance of Gradients*,
arXiv:2006.11918v4 [cs.LG].

**Source text:** `2006.11918v4.paper.txt`; `2006.11918v4.paper.html.gz`.

**Verification status: VERIFIED — the attributed claim is accurate.** Abstract page
fetched 2026-10-05; title and authors match. The external gloss — *"optimizer
replacing Adam's fixed β₂ with a per-coordinate β₂ chosen to maximize observed
gradient variance"* — is confirmed essentially word for word:

> "the running mean of squared gradient in Adam is replaced by a **weighted mean,
> with weights chosen to maximize the estimated variance of each coordinate**"

and in §4: "allow `β_t` to adopt the value that **maximizes the estimated variance of
the gradient at each iteration `t`**."

---

## 1. The actual algorithm, and what "maximize" means here

The key move is to stop thinking of the second-moment accumulator as an *estimate of
`E[g²]`* and start thinking of it as a **conservative upper bound on the local
gradient variance**, whose *weighting* you are free to choose. Given a per-coordinate
weight `β_t`, the weighted mean is a biased estimator of the second moment whose
bias is a known function of `β_t`; MaxVA picks the `β_t` that makes the implied
variance estimate **largest**, i.e. the most conservative.

The paper's own framing (§5): "Since MaxVA maximizes the variance and the gradient
converges to zero in most cases, MAdam biases towards larger `v_t` than Adam but does
not require `v_t` to be monotonically increasing, **which is like an adaptive version
of AMSGrad**." Code is released as `MAdam`; the paper's name is MaxVA.

Reported results: convergence under mild assumptions for nonconvex stochastic
optimization; image classification, machine translation and NLU; and it
"overcomes the non-convergence issue of Adam in **BERT pretraining at large batch
sizes**."

## 2. What this means for TMT

MaxVA is the shelf's direct answer to a TMT measurement that has been sitting
unexplained. `RESULTS.md` records: **Adam's second moment is non-discriminating in
TMT** — `|update|/|m|` spread only 1.0020× while `|m|` spreads 763× — i.e. Adam's
denominator is a near-constant scalar and **AdamW ≡ SGD here**.

There are exactly two possible explanations, and MaxVA is designed for the second:

1. TMT's gradients genuinely have near-constant magnitude across coordinates (then
   the denominator *should* be near-constant and nothing is wrong), or
2. Adam's *exponentially weighted* second moment is the wrong functional for
   answering "is this coordinate's gradient noisy?" in a regime where the gradient
   is drifting — MaxVA's claim is precisely that a differently-weighted mean tracks
   the local variance **faster**, "which leads to more desirable convergence
   behaviors than Adam."

MaxVA's comparison with AMSGrad is the relevant one here, because Reddi et al.
([`1904.09237v1.IDEA.md`](1904.09237v1.IDEA.md)) established that Adam's `β₂` EMA
is itself the source of non-convergence, and AMSGrad's fix (running max of `v_t`) is
crude precisely because it never decreases. TMT already trains its 8,192 `.decay`
coordinates (0.183 % of 4,481,793) under a separate rule; MaxVA is the per-coordinate
generalisation of that instinct.

Named test, in increasing cost: (a) log MaxVA's per-coordinate `β_t` and the implied
variance estimate on a short TMT run and check whether they **discriminate** where
Adam's `v_t` does not — this alone is a diagnostic result about the repo's central
measurement; (b) if they do, run MaxVA as a full arm at matched `--lr` and at the
`B ∝ ε` setting from [`1711.00489v2.IDEA.md`](1711.00489v2.IDEA.md), judged on
held-out CE + the fixed-prompt generation probe.

## 3. What this paper does NOT support

- **It does not claim MaxVA generalises better than Adam.** The claims are about
  *convergence behaviour* and avoiding "unstable or extreme adaptive learning
  rates," with results on classification, MT, NLU and BERT large-batch pretraining.
- **It does not explain *why* extreme adaptive rates are bad.** The motivation is
  empirical observation of the failure mode, and MaxVA is a fix, not a diagnosis.
  Note that the external gloss's *rationale* — "β₂ chosen to maximize observed
  gradient variance" — is a mechanism, but the paper does **not** claim the maximised
  quantity is a faithful per-coordinate noise measure. It is the input to a
  *conservative step size*, chosen for stability, **not** for statistical fidelity.
  Do not cite MaxVA as evidence that a variance-maximising `β₂` recovers noise
  information.
- **It is not about SNR, noise scale, or gradient noise temperature.**
- **No recurrent, byte-level, or long-horizon model.** BERT and ResNet-scale CNNs
  only; TMT at 4.48 M params is well outside its regime.
- **It does not interact with AdamW's decoupled weight decay**, which this paper's
  ICLR-2025 sibling ([`2411.15958v2.IDEA.md`](2411.15958v2.IDEA.md)) shows is the
  thing that stabilises Adam-family methods at high noise near the minimiser — and
  that *is* TMT's setting (AdamW, SNR 0.05).
- Nothing about memory horizons or the decay-parameter slice.

## 4. Bearing on the three open questions

- **Noise floor vs over-large step?** MaxVA is an **over-large-step / step-stability**
  intervention by construction: its whole thesis is that adaptive step sizes become
  too large late in training and that the fix is a better-weighted second moment. If
  TMT's blocker is over-large step, MaxVA is on-target; if it is a genuine floor,
  MaxVA will only make the steps smaller and change nothing else.
- **Estimator vs optimiser?** Decisively **optimiser**.
- **Input-dependent gating?** No bearing.
