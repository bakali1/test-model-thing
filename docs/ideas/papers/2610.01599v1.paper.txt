 Abstract
 Stochastic recursive momentum (STORM) achieves fast convergence for
nonconvex optimization via the variance reduction effect, but existing analyses rely on the strong average smoothness assumption. In this paper, we study the convergence of STORM for different objectives without average smoothness.
We first revisit the results under average smoothness, obtaining the $O(T^{-1/3})$ bound for nonconvex objectives and the
 $O(\sigma^{2}/(\mu T))$ bound for last-iterate output under the
 $\mu$ -Polyak–Łojasiewicz (PL) condition. Without average smoothness, we design an auxiliary sequence and compare the STORM update with it in the analysis.
With the help of this sequence, we prove that STORM still attains an $O(T^{-1/4})$ rate for nonconvex objectives, which is optimal under standard smoothness.
For convex and $\lambda$ -strongly convex objectives, we further prove
averaged and last-iterate bounds with optimal rates of
 $O(\sigma R/\sqrt{T})$ and $O(\sigma^{2}/(\lambda T))$ , respectively.
All the obtained results use the same STORM recursion with different hyperparameter choices.
 1 Introduction
 In this paper, we consider the stochastic optimization problem
 $\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(x),\qquad F(x)=\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathcal{D}}[f(x;\xi)],$ 
 (1) 
 where function $F(\cdot)$ is differentiable and $L$ -smooth. We have access to an unbiased
stochastic gradient $g(x,\xi)$ with bounded variance such that
 $\mathbb{E}_{\xi}g(x,\xi)=\nabla F(x)$ and
 $\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left\|g(x,\xi)-\nabla F(x)\right\|^{2}\leq\sigma^{2}$ .
We measure the convergence rate by the expected gradient norm $\mathbb{E}\left\|\nabla F(x)\right\|$ for the general nonconvex functions and by the
expected function gap $\mathbb{E}[F(x)]-F^{*}$ for the other cases.
 For nonconvex objectives, stochastic gradient descent (SGD) attains
an optimal $O(T^{-1/4})$ convergence rate under the smoothness assumption
 ( Ghadimi and Lan, 2013 ) . Variance reduction can further improve this
rate to $O(T^{-1/3})$ under the
additional average smoothness condition ( Fang et al., 2018 ; Arjevani et al., 2023 ) .
Among such methods, STORM ( Cutkosky and Orabona, 2019 ) is attractive
because it uses a constant batch size
per iteration and does not require periodically computing gradients with large batches.
Starting from $d_{0}=g(x_{0},\xi_{0})$ , it performs
 $\displaystyle x_{t+1}$ 
 $\displaystyle=x_{t}-\eta_{t}d_{t},$ 
 (2) 
 $\displaystyle d_{t+1}$ 
 $\displaystyle=g(x_{t+1},\xi_{t+1})+(1-a_{t+1})\bigl(d_{t}-g(x_{t},\xi_{t+1})\bigr).$ 
 (3) 
 Here $\eta_{t}$ is the stepsize and $a_{t+1}$ is the momentum parameter.
 Although STORM achieves the optimal rate for nonconvex objectives under average smoothness, many issues remain to be investigated.
First, existing analysis relies on the stronger average smoothness condition, which assumes that $\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left\|g(x,\xi)-g(y,\xi)\right\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{L}^{2}\left\|x-y\right\|^{2}$ . It remains unknown whether STORM can achieve the same $O(T^{-1/4})$ rate as SGD under standard smoothness alone. Second, existing guarantees do not establish optimal convergence rates for convex or strongly convex objectives without the average smoothness assumption.
 In this paper, we analyze these questions for the original STORM framework. First, we revisit the previous analysis under average smoothness and obtain the following results through a simpler and more transparent analysis. We do not regard the average smoothness results as the main contribution.
 • 
 With average smoothness , we first improve the previous analysis of STORM, achieving the $O(T^{-1/3})$ rate for nonconvex functions. Compared with previous analysis, we remove the logarithmic factor in the bound and avoid using large batches in the first iteration.
 • 
 With average smoothness and the $\mu$ -PL condition ,
we obtain a last-iterate bound with the convergence rate of
 $O(\sigma^{2}/(\mu T))$ via a two-phase stepsize design, allowing exponential decay of the initialization term.
 Then, we investigate the convergence of STORM without the average smoothness condition, achieving the following results, which are the main contributions of this paper.
 • 
 With standard smoothness , we prove the $O(T^{-1/4})$ convergence rate for STORM, which matches the classical SGD rate and is optimal under standard assumptions.
 • 
 For smooth and convex objectives , we obtain the weighted-mean and last-iterate bounds of order
 $\mathcal{O}(LR^{2}/T+\sigma R/\sqrt{T})$ , where $R$ is the initial distance to a minimizer.
 • 
 For smooth and $\lambda$ -strongly convex objectives , we demonstrate that the weighted-mean and last-iterate bounds can be further improved to
 $\mathcal{O}(\sigma^{2}/(\lambda T))$ .

 2 Related work
 Variance reduction for nonconvex objectives.
 SARAH ( Nguyen et al., 2017 ) first introduced a recursive estimator based on successive stochastic
gradient differences. SPIDER ( Fang et al., 2018 ) then uses this principle
to achieve $O(\varepsilon^{-3})$ stochastic gradient complexity and
 $O(n+\sqrt{n}\,\varepsilon^{-2})$ finite-sum complexity for ensuring
 $\mathbb{E}\left\|\nabla F(x)\right\|\leq\varepsilon$ , where $n$ is the number of component
functions. These guarantees assume average smoothness. SPIDER periodically
refreshes its estimator using a large batch or the full gradient.
STORM ( Cutkosky and Orabona, 2019 ) instead incorporates a fresh-gradient difference term at every step,
removing these periodic refreshes. However, the original STORM analysis contains logarithmic factors in the rate.
In particular, its guarantee is
 $O(\log(T)T^{-2/3})$ in expected squared gradient norm, hence
 $O(\sqrt{\log(T)}T^{-1/3})$ in expected norm. 1 1 
 1 
 In
 Cutkosky and Orabona (2019) , the last simplification in Theorem 1’s
proof drops a factor $\sqrt{M}$ uncarefully, where $M=O(\log(T+2))$ . Furthermore, their Appendix B, Theorem 2, explicitly retains
the logarithmic factor for the nonadaptive guarantee. Other variants of STORM have also been investigated in the literature. STORM+
 ( Levy et al., 2021 ) obtains
 $O(T^{-1/3})$ with adaptive parameters under the extra assumption of
bounded gradients and bounded objective value. Ada-STORM ( Jiang et al., 2024 ) attains the same rates without additional assumptions but with a large initial batchsize on the order $\mathcal{O}(T^{1/3})$ .
 STORM-based methods for convex optimization.
 The STORM estimator has also been studied beyond nonconvex
optimization.
 Zhang et al. (2020) combine the STORM estimator with
Frank–Wolfe updates and obtain an $O(T^{-1/2})$ expected
function-gap bound for convex objectives.
However, their analysis requires a bounded feasible set, samplewise smoothness, and bounded stochastic gradients.
More recently, Dahan and Levy (2025) combine STORM with
anytime averaging in $\mu^{2}$ -SGD and attain the
 $O(T^{-1/2})$ rate.
Their method evaluates gradients at running averages of a
separate projected sequence, and its analysis uses samplewise
smoothness and a bounded feasible set.
 Variance reduction under the $\mu$ -PL condition.
 The variance reduction technique PAGE ( Li et al., 2021 ) obtains an expected function gap of at most $\varepsilon$ with
 $O((B+\sqrt{B}\,\mathcal{L}/\mu)\log(2\Delta_{0}/\varepsilon))$ 
stochastic gradient evaluations, where
 $B=\lceil 2\sigma^{2}/(\mu\varepsilon)\rceil$ . This result assumes the average smoothness and bounded variance, and its refresh batch depends on the target accuracy.
For compositional objectives, RECOVER ( Qi et al., 2021 ) obtains the sample complexity
 $O(\max\{\mu^{-3/2}\varepsilon^{-1/2},\mu^{-1}\varepsilon^{-1}\})$ .
For stochastic multi-level compositional optimization,
 Jiang et al. (2022) develop stage-wise SMVR using
STORM estimators.
For objectives satisfying the $\mu$ -PL condition, their
stage-wise analysis gives complexity
 $O(\mu^{-1}\varepsilon^{-1}+\mu^{-3/2}\varepsilon^{-1/2})$ ,
which reduces to $O((\mu\varepsilon)^{-1})$ 
when $\varepsilon\leq\mu$ .

 4 Convergence under Average Smoothness
 We first revisit the convergence results under average smoothness (Assumption 3 ). We begin
with the general nonconvex objectives, then use the PL condition to obtain the function-gap guarantee.
 4.1 Nonconvex Objectives
 For the STORM algorithm, the estimator $d_{t}$ tracks the true function gradient $\nabla F(x_{t})$ , and we define
 $q_{t}=\mathbb{E}\left\|d_{t}-\nabla F(x_{t})\right\|^{2}$ for its mean squared error. Average smoothness bounds
the movement error by a term proportional to
 $\mathcal{L}^{2}\eta_{t}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left\|d_{t}\right\|^{2}$ via $\mathbb{E}\left\|g(x_{t+1},\xi_{t+1})-g(x_{t},\xi_{t+1})\right\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{L}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left\|x_{t+1}-x_{t}\right\|^{2}$ . The descent inequality also contains a
negative term involving $\mathbb{E}\left\|d_{t}\right\|^{2}$ . To cancel them, we
use the potential
 $\Psi_{t}=\mathbb{E}[F(x_{t})-F^{*}]+\eta q_{t}/(2a_{t})$ to obtain
 $\Psi_{t+1}-\Psi_{t}\leq-\frac{\eta}{2}\mathbb{E}\left\|\nabla F(x_{t})\right\|^{2}+\eta a_{t+1}\sigma^{2}.$

to the following bound.

 4.2 Objectives Satisfying the PL Condition
 The PL inequality converts the negative gradient-norm term in the
descent bound into a decrease proportional to the current function
gap, i.e., $-\left\|\nabla F(x_{t})\right\|^{2}\leq-2\mu(F(x_{t})-F^{*})$ . In this case, we set the potential
 $V_{t}=\mathbb{E}[F(x_{t})-F^{*}]+q_{t}/\mu$ and couple the parameters through
 $a_{t+1}=\mu\eta_{t}$ . The descent and tracking inequalities will then give
 $V_{t+1}\leq(1-\mu\eta_{t})V_{t}+2\mu\eta_{t}^{2}\sigma^{2}\quad\text{when }\eta_{t}\leq\mu/(8\mathcal{L}^{2}).$ 
 (11)

 The first hyperparameter schedule gives quadratic decay of the initialization

It carries $(x_{t},d_{t})$ between phases without restarting and introduces no extra non-constant factor in the leading stochastic term.

 Core of the analysis.
 We have already proved $V_{t+1}\leq(1-\mu\eta_{t})V_{t}+2\mu\eta_{t}^{2}\sigma^{2}$ in the previous analysis. When using $\mu\eta_{t}=2/(s+t)$ , we have:
 $\prod_{j=k+1}^{T-1}\left(1-\frac{2}{j+s}\right)=\frac{(k+s-1)(k+s)}{(T+s-2)(T+s-1)}.$ 
 The initial error is thus multiplied by
 $O(s^{2}/(T+s)^{2})$ , while each noise term’s $(k+s)^{-2}$ factor
is canceled, up to constants, by its product weight $(k+s-1)(k+s)$ . Their sum gives
 $O(\sigma^{2}/[\mu(T+s)])$ . In the first phase of the alternative
schedule, by using a constant learning rate $\mu\eta_{t}=2/s$ , the inequality $(1-2/s)^{m}\leq e^{-2m/s}$ gives exponential decay.
 5 Convergence without Average Smoothness
 Without the average smoothness, nearby query points may not
have nearby stochastic gradients. The main difficulty is the
conditional bias of the estimator $d_{t}$ . Denote $e_{t}=d_{t}-\nabla F(x_{t})$ . We
separate this inherited error from a centered increment $\nu_{t}$ via
 $e_{t}=\beta e_{t-1}+\nu_{t}$ .
Bounded variance can control $\mathbb{E}\left\|e_{t}\right\|^{2}$ , but this alone would leave a nonvanishing term in the usual descent inequality.
 We handle the inherited error in two ways. For nonconvex objectives,
we shift the iterate by a multiple of $e_{t-1}$ , so that the resulting
recursion contains only the centered noise. Standard smoothness then
controls the difference between the two gradient evaluation points.
For convex objectives, we compare STORM with an auxiliary
sequence driven by the same $\nu_{t}$ . The noise then cancels in their
difference, which can be controlled by nonexpansiveness, or by
contraction under strong convexity.

 Stepsize blocks.
 Note that we also want to derive the rates for the last iterate output, and an average guarantee does not by itself control $F(x_{T})$ .
We therefore arrange the stepsizes so that a good value in an early
block controls values in the following shorter block. The blocks
shrink geometrically toward $T$ , and the stepsize is halved at each
boundary. Larger early steps reduce the initial error and smaller late

 7 Conclusion
 In this paper, we studied the convergence of STORM under different
objective geometries and smoothness assumptions.
Under average smoothness, we provided a direct analysis of the
logarithm-free nonconvex rate and established a last-iterate
guarantee with an optimal stochastic term under the PL condition.
Under standard smoothness alone, STORM matches the nonconvex
SGD rate and attains optimal stochastic orders for both averaged
and last iterates in the convex and strongly convex settings.
These guarantees follow from different parameter choices within
the same STORM recursion, with a single initial sample and a
constant number of gradient evaluations per iteration.

 All distances in this calculation have finite expectations: indeed,
 $y_{t}-y_{p}=-\sum_{j=p}^{t-1}\eta_{j}h_{j}$ is a finite sum of random vectors
with finite second moments. 
 Comparison of adjacent blocks. 
Use the comparison blocks ( 62 ). They are
consecutive from $M-1$ through $T$ , and $B_{m}=\{T\}$ .
Choose index $\tau_{i}\in B_{i}$ minimizing
 $\mathbb{E}F(y_{t})$ on $B_{i}$ , and write 
 $H_{i}=\mathbb{E}F(y_{\tau_{i+1}})-\mathbb{E}F(y_{\tau_{i}})\qquad(0\leq i 
 The index $\tau_{i}$ is deterministic because the quantities being
minimized are expectations, not observed sample-path values. 
 Apply ( 79 ) with
 $p=\tau_{i}$ and $q=\max B_{i+1}$ . The left sum consists of the
remaining part of the source block $B_{i}$ and all of the target block
 $B_{i+1}$ . On the source part, the choice of $\tau_{i}$ gives 
 $\mathbb{E}F(y_{t})-\mathbb{E}F(y_{\tau_{i}})\geq 0.$ 
 On the target block, the choice of $\tau_{i+1}$ gives 
 $\mathbb{E}F(y_{t})-\mathbb{E}F(y_{\tau_{i}})\geq\mathbb{E}F(y_{\tau_{i+1}})-\mathbb{E}F(y_{\tau_{i}})=H_{i}.$ 
 All stepsizes are positive. Thus the whole left side of
( 79 ) is at least
 $2H_{i}\sum_{t\in B_{i+1}}\eta_{t}$ . Dividing by this positive
weight sum gives 
 $H_{i}\leq\frac{G^{2}\sum_{t=\tau_{i}}^{\max B_{i+1}}\eta_{t}^{2}}{2\sum_{t\in B_{i+1}}\eta_{t}}.$ 
 (80) 
 This reasoning is valid whether $H_{i}$ is positive, zero, or negative. 
 The effect of rounded block lengths. 
First consider $i=0$ . By ( 64 ), the numerator
in ( 80 ) contains at most $3|B_{1}|$ terms.
Furthermore, $T+1\leq 4M$ and $s\geq 0$ give 
 $\eta_{t}\leq\gamma_{M-1}=\frac{c}{M+s}\leq 4\gamma_{T}\quad\text{throughout the first comparison span}.$ 
 On $B_{1}$ , the multiplier is $1/2$ , and $t\leq T$ implies
 $\gamma_{t}\geq\gamma_{T}$ . Each target stepsize is therefore
at least $\gamma_{T}/2$ . Substituting these upper and lower
bounds into ( 80 ) yields 
 $H_{0}\leq\frac{3|B_{1}|G^{2}(4\gamma_{T})^{2}}{2|B_{1}|(\gamma_{T}/2)}=48G^{2}\gamma_{T}.$ 
 Next take $1\leq i 64 ), the
comparison span contains at most $4|B_{i+1}|$ indices.
All its indices satisfy $t\geq U_{1}=K\geq(T+1)/2$ , whence 
 $\gamma_{t}=\frac{c}{t+s+1}\leq 2\gamma_{T}.$ 
 The largest multiplier in this span is $2^{-i}$ , so its largest
stepsize is at most $2^{1-i}\gamma_{T}$ .
On the target block the multiplier is $2^{-i-1}$ , and every
stepsize is at least $2^{-i-1}\gamma_{T}$ . Consequently, 
 $H_{i}\leq\frac{4|B_{i+1}|G^{2}(2^{1-i}\gamma_{T})^{2}}{2|B_{i+1}|2^{-i-1}\gamma_{T}}=16\,2^{-i}G^{2}\gamma_{T}.$ 
 When $m=1$ , there are no comparisons of this second type. 
 Summation over the blocks. 
Since $\tau_{m}=T$ , consecutive differences telescope: 
 $\displaystyle\mathbb{E}F(y_{T})-\mathbb{E}F(y_{\tau_{0}})$ 
 $\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}H_{i}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\left(48+16\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}2^{-i}\right)G^{2}\gamma_{T}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq 64G^{2}\gamma_{T}.$ 
 The defining property of $\tau_{0}$ now proves
( 78 ). The last comparison uses the update at
 $t=T$ only to produce the nonnegative terminal distance
 $\mathbb{E}\left\|y_{T+1}-y_{\tau_{m-1}}\right\|^{2}$ , which is discarded.
The point $y_{T+1}$ need not be returned or computed by the algorithm.
∎ 
 H.3 Proof of the last-iterate theorem
 Proof of Theorem 8 .
 Apply Lemma 5 . In particular, the companion
starts at $y_{0}=x_{0}$ and has centered innovations satisfying 
 $\mathbb{E}[\nu_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]=0,\qquad\mathbb{E}[\left\|\nu_{t}\right\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq S^{2},\qquad S^{2}=(1+\beta)^{2}\sigma^{2}\leq\frac{9}{4}\sigma^{2}.$ 
 Write 
 $D_{t}=\mathbb{E}\left\|y_{t}-x^{*}\right\|^{2},\qquad\delta_{t}=\mathbb{E}[F(y_{t})-F^{*}],\qquad\gamma_{t}=\frac{2}{\lambda(t+s+1)},$ 
 and let $M=\lceil(T+1)/4\rceil$ , $K=\lceil(T+1)/2\rceil$ .
The schedule ( 34 ) satisfies $\eta_{t}=\gamma_{t}$ for $0\leq t 
 Distance decrease in the first half. 
Expand one companion update around $x^{*}$ : 
 $y_{t+1}-x^{*}=y_{t}-x^{*}-\eta_{t}\nabla F(y_{t})-\eta_{t}\nu_{t}.$ 
 The first three terms are $\mathcal{F}_{t}$ -measurable, so the
cross term with $\nu_{t}$ vanishes after taking expectation.
Gradient-map contraction and $\nabla F(x^{*})=0$ then imply 
 $\displaystyle D_{t+1}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda\eta_{t})^{2}D_{t}+\eta_{t}^{2}S^{2}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda\eta_{t})D_{t}+\eta_{t}^{2}S^{2}.$ 
 (81) 
 The second line follows from $0\leq\lambda\eta_{t}\leq 1$ .
During the first block, where $\eta_{t}=\gamma_{t}$ , we claim that 
 $D_{t}\leq\frac{(s+1)^{2}R^{2}}{(t+s+1)^{2}}+\frac{4S^{2}}{\lambda^{2}(t+s+1)},\qquad 0\leq t\leq K.$ 
 (82) 
 At $t=0$ , the first term equals $D_{0}=R^{2}$ and the second is
nonnegative. For the induction step, put $u=t+s+1$ .
Here $u\geq 5$ , so $1-2/u\geq 0$ and substitution of the induction
upper bound into ( 81 ) preserves the inequality.
Since $\eta_{t}=2/(\lambda u)$ , this gives 
 $\displaystyle D_{t+1}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{2}{u}\right)\left(\frac{(s+1)^{2}R^{2}}{u^{2}}+\frac{4S^{2}}{\lambda^{2}u}\right)+\frac{4S^{2}}{\lambda^{2}u^{2}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\frac{(s+1)^{2}R^{2}(u-2)}{u^{3}}+\frac{4S^{2}(u-1)}{\lambda^{2}u^{2}}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\frac{(s+1)^{2}R^{2}}{(u+1)^{2}}+\frac{4S^{2}}{\lambda^{2}(u+1)}.$ 
 The last line follows term by term from 
 $(u-2)(u+1)^{2}=u^{3}-3u-2\leq u^{3},\qquad(u-1)(u+1)=u^{2}-1\leq u^{2}.$ 
 This proves ( 82 ). 
 Second moments of the search directions. 
For any $t\geq M-1$ , discard contraction in
( 81 ) and sum from $M-1$ to $t-1$ : 
 $D_{t}\leq D_{M-1}+S^{2}\sum_{j=M-1}^{t-1}\eta_{j}^{2}\leq D_{M-1}+S^{2}\sum_{j=M-1}^{\infty}\gamma_{j}^{2}.$ 
 The infinite sum is only an upper bound on a finite sum; it does
not introduce additional algorithmic iterations. By monotonicity
of the summand and $M+s\geq 1$ , 
 $\displaystyle\sum_{j=M-1}^{\infty}\gamma_{j}^{2}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\lambda^{2}}\sum_{k=M}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k+s)^{2}}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\lambda^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{(M+s)^{2}}+\int_{M}^{\infty}\frac{du}{(u+s)^{2}}\right)$ 
 $\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\lambda^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{(M+s)^{2}}+\frac{1}{M+s}\right)\leq\frac{8}{\lambda^{2}(M+s)}.$ 
 Use ( 82 ) at $t=M-1$ to conclude that 
 $D_{t}\leq\frac{(s+1)^{2}R^{2}}{(M+s)^{2}}+\frac{12S^{2}}{\lambda^{2}(M+s)},\qquad M-1\leq t\leq T.$ 
 (83) 
 Now write $h_{t}=\nabla F(y_{t})+\nu_{t}$ for the companion direction.
Since $y_{t}$ is $\mathcal{F}_{t}$ -measurable, conditional centering gives
 $\mathbb{E}\left\langle\nabla F(y_{t}),\nu_{t}\right\rangle=0$ .
Standard smoothness and $\nabla F(x^{*})=0$ imply
 $\left\|\nabla F(y_{t})\right\|\leq L\left\|y_{t}-x^{*}\right\|$ .
Consequently, 
 $\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|h_{t}\right\|^{2}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left\|\nabla F(y_{t})\right\|^{2}+\mathbb{E}\left\|\nu_{t}\right\|^{2}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq L^{2}D_{t}+S^{2}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\frac{L^{2}(s+1)^{2}R^{2}}{(M+s)^{2}}+\left(1+\frac{12\kappa^{2}}{M+s}\right)S^{2}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\frac{L^{2}(s+1)^{2}R^{2}}{(M+s)^{2}}+4S^{2}=:G^{2}.$ 
 For the last line, $M+s\geq s\geq 4\kappa^{2}$ gives
 $12\kappa^{2}/(M+s)\leq 3$ . Thus the moment assumption of
Lemma 16 holds on the required trajectory segment.
This argument uses smoothness of $F$ only; it imposes neither
sample smoothness nor a globally bounded stochastic gradient. 
 A reference function value. 
For $M-1\leq t\leq K-1$ , the stepsize is $\gamma_{t}$ .
Lemma 11 applies because $\gamma_{t}\leq 1/(2L)$ ,
and gives 
 $D_{t+1}\leq D_{t}-\gamma_{t}\delta_{t}+\gamma_{t}^{2}S^{2}.$ 
 Rearranging and summing makes the interior distances cancel: 
 $\sum_{t=M-1}^{K-1}\gamma_{t}\delta_{t}\leq D_{M-1}-D_{K}+S^{2}\sum_{t=M-1}^{K-1}\gamma_{t}^{2}\leq D_{M-1}+S^{2}\sum_{t=M-1}^{K-1}\gamma_{t}^{2}.$ 
 The left side is at least the smallest $\delta_{t}$ in the range
times the positive sum of weights. Hence 
 $\min_{M-1\leq t\leq K-1}\delta_{t}\leq\frac{D_{M-1}+S^{2}\sum_{t=M-1}^{K-1}\gamma_{t}^{2}}{\sum_{t=M-1}^{K-1}\gamma_{t}}.$ 
 There are $n=K-M+1\geq(T+1)/4$ indices. Their largest denominator
in $\gamma_{t}$ is $K+s$ and their smallest is $M+s$ , so 
 $\sum_{t=M-1}^{K-1}\gamma_{t}\geq\frac{2n}{\lambda(K+s)},\qquad\sum_{t=M-1}^{K-1}\gamma_{t}^{2}\leq\frac{4n}{\lambda^{2}(M+s)^{2}}.$ 
 Also $K\leq 2M$ , and therefore $K+s\leq 2(M+s)$ . It follows that 
 $\displaystyle\min_{M-1\leq t\leq K-1}\delta_{t}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda(K+s)}{2n}D_{M-1}+\frac{2S^{2}(K+s)}{\lambda(M+s)^{2}}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\lambda(M+s)}{T+1}D_{M-1}+\frac{4S^{2}}{\lambda(M+s)}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\lambda(s+1)^{2}R^{2}}{(T+1)(M+s)}+\frac{16S^{2}}{\lambda(T+1)}+\frac{4S^{2}}{\lambda(M+s)}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\lambda(s+1)^{2}R^{2}}{(T+1)(M+s)}+\frac{32S^{2}}{\lambda(T+1)}.$ 
 In the third line, we use the sharper bound
( 82 ) at $M-1$ , whose noise coefficient is $4$ ,
rather than the tail bound with coefficient $12$ .
For the last line, use $M+s\geq(T+1)/4$ . 
 The last companion iterate. 
Lemma 16 , with $c=2/\lambda$ , adds at most 
 $\displaystyle 64G^{2}\gamma_{T}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\frac{128L^{2}(s+1)^{2}R^{2}}{\lambda(T+s+1)(M+s)^{2}}+\frac{512S^{2}}{\lambda(T+s+1)}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\frac{32\lambda(s+1)^{2}R^{2}}{(T+1)(M+s)}+\frac{512S^{2}}{\lambda(T+1)}.$ 
 For the initialization term, divide its coefficient by the
coefficient of $\lambda(s+1)^{2}R^{2}/[(T+1)(M+s)]$ : 
 $\frac{128\kappa^{2}(T+1)}{(T+s+1)(M+s)}=128\frac{\kappa^{2}}{M+s}\frac{T+1}{T+s+1}\leq 32.$ 
 Combining this with the preceding bound on the minimum over
 $M-1\leq t\leq K-1$ , and using $4+32=36$ and
 $32+512=544$ , gives 
 $\mathbb{E}[F(y_{T})-F^{*}]\leq\frac{36\lambda(s+1)^{2}R^{2}}{(T+1)(M+s)}+\frac{544S^{2}}{\lambda(T+1)}.$

 Transfer to STORM. 
The comparison bound ( 58 ) gives 
 $\mathbb{E}[F(x_{T})-F^{*}]\leq 2\mathbb{E}[F(y_{T})-F^{*}]+\frac{3L\beta^{2}\sigma^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}.$ 
 Substitute ( 84 ), then use
 $S^{2}\leq 9\sigma^{2}/4$ and $\beta=1/(T+s+1)$ : 
 $\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[F(x_{T})-F^{*}]$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\frac{72\lambda(s+1)^{2}R^{2}}{(T+1)(M+s)}+\frac{1088S^{2}}{\lambda(T+1)}+\frac{3\kappa\sigma^{2}}{\lambda(T+s+1)^{2}}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\frac{72\lambda(s+1)^{2}R^{2}}{(T+1)(M+s)}+\frac{2448\sigma^{2}}{\lambda(T+1)}+\frac{3\kappa\sigma^{2}}{\lambda(T+s+1)^{2}}.$ 
 Finally, $s\geq 4\kappa^{2}\geq\kappa$ and
 $(T+s+1)^{2}\geq 4(T+1)s\geq\kappa(T+1)$ imply 
 $\frac{3\kappa\sigma^{2}}{\lambda(T+s+1)^{2}}\leq\frac{3\sigma^{2}}{\lambda(T+1)}.$ 
 Finally, $M+s\geq(T+1)/4$ , so $72/[(T+1)(M+s)]\leq 288/(T+1)^{2}$ , which proves the bound in Theorem 8 .
