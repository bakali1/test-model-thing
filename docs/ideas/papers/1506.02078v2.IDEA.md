## 1. The two results TMT actually uses

1. **§4.2 — interpretable cells exist and outlive the BPTT horizon.** The paper is
   explicit that this is *not* explained by the gradient signal: "truncated
   backpropagation with our hyperparameters prevents the gradient signal from
   directly noticing dependencies longer than 100 characters, but we still observe
   cells that reliably keep track of quotes or comment blocks much longer than 100
   characters." The paper's own hypothesis: these cells develop on short patterns
   and then generalise. **This is the strongest published evidence that a memory
   horizon can exceed the training gradient horizon by ~2.3×** — TMT's 8–32 B
   probed horizon against BPTT-100 is the same phenomenon, and Karpathy is the
   precedent that it is not automatically a failure.
2. **§4 — n-gram comparison as the ruler.** The comparative analysis against finite
   horizon n-gram models "traces the source of the LSTM improvements to long-range
   structural dependencies." §4 also does error attribution by category. TMT has
   built the Fig. 5 distance curve and the n-gram → dynamic-memory ladder into
   `tools/eval.py`; this paper is the source of that instrument.

Also available and unused by TMT: **§4.2 gate-saturation statistics** (gate counted
left/right-saturated at activation <0.1 or >0.9), which is exactly the "gate
saturation statistics" lever the roadmap names at line 293.

## 2. What this means for TMT

**TMT's 4,481,793 parameters sit between Karpathy's 400 k and 1.3 M single-layer
LSTMs, and TMT's byte vocabulary (256) is 2.5–3× Karpathy's (87 / 101).** That
makes the comparison unusually well-matched, and it makes two of TMT's numbers
directly comparable rather than merely analogous:

- Karpathy's largest model, ~1.3 M params, batch 100, RMSProp, base lr 2e-3,
  unroll 100 → a quote cell tracking ~230 chars. TMT: 4.48 M params, byte level,
  an 8–32 B probed horizon. **The ceiling to quote is ~230 chars, not 8–32 — and
  it is a ceiling for a model with a 100-step gradient horizon, so TMT's horizon is
  not being compared at equal BPTT.** If TMT's BPTT is shorter than 100, the
  comparison is unfair *to TMT*; if longer, TMT's 8–32 B horizon is worse than the
  reference at equal setup.
- Karpathy used **RMSProp**, not Adam. Given `RESULTS.md`'s finding that AdamW ≡ SGD
  in TMT with a constant normalizer, the historically relevant optimiser baseline
  for a small char-level LSTM is RMSProp. That is an untested TMT arm, and
  [`1705.08292v2.IDEA.md`](1705.08292v2.IDEA.md) gives the reason to expect a
  difference.

Named test: report the TMT BPTT alongside every horizon number, and quote the
~230-char figure only with its BPTT-100 attached. The roadmap already does this
(ROADMAP line 127); this note makes the requirement explicit so it survives into
the ideas directory.

## 3. What this paper does NOT support

- **It does not set a 230-character *upper* bound.** It is one cell in one 1.3 M
  model on Wikipedia/LaTeX, read off a figure. The paper's own explanation
  ("we hypothesize…") is speculative. Do not convert "~230 chars" into a ceiling
  law for TMT; the roadmap already treats it as a "ceiling", which is stronger than
  the paper supports.
- **The number is a figure, not a table.** The text extract beside this file has
  **no figures**; Figure 2 is the source of "~230 characters". Anyone re-checking
  this must read the original PDF.
- **It does not claim 1.3 M parameters is enough.** It is one point in a sweep
  (50K/130K/400K/1.3M) on a character vocabulary of 87/101 and a 6.2 M-character
  corpus. TMT's vocabulary is 256 and its corpus is far larger; the parameter
  transfer is a sanity check, not a scaling law.
- **It says nothing about the optimiser's second moment, SNR, noise scale,
  learning-rate schedules, or gradient noise.** §3.3 is one sentence of
  optimisation settings.
- **It says nothing about decay parameters.** TMT's 8,192 `.decay` coordinates
  (0.183 % of 4,481,793) have no counterpart in Karpathy's LSTM; do not map one
  onto the other.
- **"Interpretable cells" is a 2015 read of t-SNE/activation plots**, offered as
  suggestive rather than as a controlled causal result. TMT should not treat "a
  cell exists" as evidence that a mechanism is being used for the claimed purpose.
- No byte-level model (the vocabularies are ASCII), no Adam, no SSM, no modern
  recurrent architecture.

## 4. Bearing on the three open questions

- **Noise floor vs over-large step?** No bearing. (Its optimiser was RMSProp at a
  tuned base LR 2e-3 — a reminder that the LR was tuned, which TMT's never was.)
- **Estimator vs optimiser?** No bearing, but it supplies the baseline optimiser
  family (RMSProp) that TMT has never run.
- **Input-dependent gating?** The closest thing on the shelf to a positive
  architecture result: a **gated** LSTM attains a >2× over-horizon memory from
  truncated BPTT, without input-dependent gating of any novel kind. Combined with
  [`2206.11893v2.IDEA.md`](2206.11893v2.IDEA.md) (a **non-gated** diagonal SSM does
  long-range work when initialised well), the shelf's architecture evidence says:
  *check the parameterisation and the memory-unit semantics before adding gates.*
