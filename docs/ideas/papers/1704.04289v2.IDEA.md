# Paper notes — "Stochastic Gradient Descent as Approximate Bayesian Inference" (arXiv:1704.04289v2)

**Paper:** Stephan Mandt, Matthew D. Hoffman, David M. Blei — *Stochastic Gradient
Descent as Approximate Bayesian Inference*, arXiv:1704.04289v2 [stat.ML, cs.LG],
JMLR 18 (2017) 1–35 (published version).

**Source text:** `1704.04289v2.paper.txt`; `1704.04289v2.paper.html.gz`.

**Verification status: VERIFIED.** Abstract page fetched 2026-10-05; title and
authors match; journal-ref `Journal of Machine Learning Research 18 (2017) 1-35`.
The attributed claim — *"the mathematical 'diffusion' interpretation"* — is
supported: §1 opens "Stochastic Gradient Descent with a constant learning rate
(constant SGD) simulates a Markov chain with a stationary distribution," and the
paper derives that stationary distribution's **covariance** in closed form
(precision-matrix / Hessian form, with an inverse-Lipschitz-squared scale), plus an
Ornstein–Uhlenbeck treatment (§7.1, "Confirming the Stationary Distribution's
Covariance").

**Status of this note:** external ideas, not a plan and not a result.

---

## 1. What it actually gives

Five results, in the paper's own numbering:

1. Constant SGD as approximate Bayesian posterior inference: choose its tuning
   parameters to minimise the **KL divergence** between its stationary distribution
   and the posterior.
2. A **variational EM** algorithm that emerges from constant SGD, optimising
   hyperparameters in complex probabilistic models.
3. **SGD with momentum for sampling**, with the damping coefficient adjusted
   accordingly.
4. Approximation-error analysis for Langevin Dynamics and Stochastic Gradient Fisher
   Scoring **due to finite learning rates**.
5. A short proof of the optimality of **Polyak averaging**, and a new Averaged
   Stochastic Gradient Sampler.

The mechanism that matters for TMT is result (1)/(§3): the stationary covariance is
set by the **preconditioner / inverse curvature**, and the learning rate sets the
"temperature." In plain language: **a constant learning rate makes SGD explore a
distribution whose width is lr ÷ curvature.** That is the mathematical form of
"over-large step = high noise temperature," and it is exactly the lever
McCandlish et al. and Smith et al. turn empirically.

## 2. What this means for TMT

This paper is the *justification* for reading TMT's two SNR numbers as one
quantity. `RESULTS.md` reports gradient SNR ≈ 0.05 while training and **0.633 at a
frozen θ**, and concludes "diagnosis is now over-large step, not a noise floor."
Mandt et al. supply the sentence that makes that conclusion defensible: with a
constant LR, the stationary covariance — hence the ratio of noise to signal the
repo's estimator is measuring — **is the learning rate divided by the curvature**.
A 12.6× gap between a moving θ and a frozen θ is exactly what you would expect if
the dominant term is `η·H^{-1}` and the iterate is moving.

Named test, cheap and decisive: hold the data order fixed and sweep global `--lr`
over ≥3 decades (never done in this repo, per `RESULTS.md`). If the Mandt model
holds, SNR should scale ≈ linearly in `η` and the frozen-θ value 0.633 should be the
`η → 0` intercept. A null result (SNR flat in `η`) falsifies the diffusion reading
and points at the estimator. Combine with the `B ∝ ε` rule in
[`1711.00489v2.IDEA.md`](1711.00489v2.IDEA.md): varying `η` at fixed `B`, then
varying `B` at fixed `η`, separates the two axes.

Result (3) is separately relevant because TMT's recurrent trace units are a form of
momentum-like state, and this paper gives the sampling-theoretic correction for
damped momentum rather than leaving it heuristic.

## 3. What this paper does NOT support

- **It is about constant-LR SGD, not Adam.** The paper's stationary-distribution
  derivation is for SGD (with a preconditioner). Nothing here justifies, or
  predicts, the behaviour of Adam's per-coordinate denominator. TMT's measurement
  that Adam's second moment is non-discriminating (constant normalizer) is
  *outside* this model.
- **It assumes the loss is convex** (or at least uses a Hessian / precision matrix
  argument). TMT's 16-layer byte-level model is nonconvex; `H^{-1}` is a local
  quantity and the stationary distribution of SGD on a nonconvex loss need not
  exist. The paper does not license "our SNR is a temperature" in TMT's regime
  without saying so.
- It does **not** talk about SNR as a *measurable statistic*. It provides no
  estimator and no procedure. McCandlish et al. supply that; this paper supplies the
  interpretation.
- It does **not** address recurrent nets, byte-level modelling, memory horizons, or
  any architectural question. Its examples are logistic regression, a Bayesian
  logistic-regression/movie-review model, and a small convnet/DBN on MNIST.
- Result (1)'s "tuning to match a posterior" requires a **specified posterior** —
  which TMT does not have. Read it as a variational framing, not as a training rule.
- It is **2017** and says nothing about adaptive methods, AdamW, or modern
  LLM-scale training.

## 4. Bearing on the three open questions

- **Noise floor vs over-large step?** The strongest *mechanistic* support on the
  shelf for **over-large step**, because it supplies the `η / curvature` law that
  predicts the observed 12.6× frozen-vs-moving gap. It also makes the question
  falsifiable with an LR sweep.
- **Estimator vs optimiser?** Says the interpretation is optimiser-side (the step
  size), which is why the LR sweep is the first thing to run.
- **Input-dependent gating?** No bearing.
