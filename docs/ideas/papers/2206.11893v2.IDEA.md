# Paper notes — "On the Parameterization and Initialization of Diagonal State Space Models" / S4D (arXiv:2206.11893v2)

**Paper:** Albert Gu, Ankit Gupta, Karan Goel, Christopher Ré — *On the
Parameterization and Initialization of Diagonal State Space Models*, arXiv:2206.11893v2
[cs.LG], 23 Jun 2022.

**Source text:** `2206.11893v2.paper.txt`; `2206.11893v2.paper.html.gz`.

**Verification status: VERIFIED — the attributed claim is supported, in the
abstract and in the body.** Abstract page fetched 2026-10-05; title and authors
match. The claim *"diagonal SSMs are very sensitive to how timescales are
parameterised and initialised"* is confirmed twice: the abstract says "we show
that the initialization is critical for performance," and §4 says "these should be
spread out, which explains why **random initializations of `A` do not perform
well**," with §5.2 reporting controlled ablations showing "simple variants can
substantially degrade performance."

**Status of this note:** external ideas, not a plan and not a result.

---

## 1. The results that matter here

- Almost all SSMs have an equivalent **diagonal** form algebraically — but the paper
  stresses the diagonal restriction "may not represent all SSMs **numerically**, and
  finding a good initialization is critical."
- The diagonal restriction of S4's HiPPO matrix "surprisingly recovers the same
  kernel in the limit of infinite state dimension" — i.e. the parameterisation is
  not an approximation if you get the spectrum right.
- Complex-valued `A` is required: "it is critical to use complex-valued matrices in
  order to use diagonal SSMs."
- The timescales must be **spread out** on the log-time axis, which is precisely
  why random initialisation fails.
- §7 (and the App. B robustness study): "the SSM basis is surprisingly sensitive to
  low-rank perturbations."
- S4D is 2 lines of kernel code and averages 85 % on Long Range Arena.

## 2. What this means for TMT

This is the shelf's best-motivated analogue to TMT's memory blocker, and the
analogy is tight enough to be worth writing down precisely.

TMT's measured state (`RESULTS.md`): the state probe finds the memory horizon at
**8 bytes** (cue linearly decadable at d=8, null at d≥32, bracket [8, 32] B), while
the mean half-life is **1.14 B** and 7 of 8,192 dims exceed 8 B. The repo's own
diagnosis is that "the decay histogram does not predict memory" — the half-life
*understates* the horizon 7× and the `max σ^d > 1e-7` tail *overstates* it 128×,
because the tail is a per-coordinate amplitude while the probe reads an
8192-dim state.

S4D says three things that map onto that:

1. **The relevant object is the spread of the timescale spectrum, not any single
   timescale statistic.** TMT currently has three disagreeing horizon numbers
   (1.14 B mean half-life, 8 B probed, a σ-tail implying ~1000 B). S4D's position is
   that a *distribution* over decay rates is the thing, and a summary statistic of
   it (mean half-life, max) is the wrong thing to compare against a probe. This is a
   direct methodological endorsement of the repo's own "brackets the truth badly"
   note.
2. **Random / ad-hoc initialisation of the decay spectrum is a known failure mode,
   not a neutral default.** TMT's `.decay` coordinates are 8,192 of 4,481,793 params
   (0.183 %) and were presumably set by a scheme that has never been ablated against
   a spread-out alternative. S4D predicts that a spread-out log-spaced
   initialisation of the 8,192 decay timescales beats whatever is there now, at
   matched everything else.
3. **"Surprisingly sensitive to low-rank perturbations" is a warning about the
   architecture's margin.** TMT's recurrent trace units and SSM-like state are
   diagonal, low-rank-flavoured constructions. S4D says that class of basis has thin
   margins.

Named test: a from-scratch arm whose 8,192 `.decay` timescales are initialised
log-spaced over the TMT-relevant range (chosen to bracket the measured 8–32 B
horizon) versus the current initialisation, byte-matched, judged on held-out CE and
the state-probe horizon. This is the cheapest architecture-side experiment the
shelf nominates, and it is orthogonal to the LR question in
[`1711.00489v2.IDEA.md`](1711.00489v2.IDEA.md) — so the two can be run as separate
arms rather than confounded.

## 3. What this paper does NOT support

- **It does not say a diagonal SSM can hold long-range memory cheaply at TMT's
  scale.** S4D's evaluation is Long Range Arena (85 %), image, audio, and medical
  time series at ~7M–300M parameters, with **convolutional scanning** across long
  sequences. TMT is a 4.48 M-parameter autoregressive byte model with an 8–32 B
  horizon and 890 k steps. Nothing transfers quantitatively.
- **It does not claim input-dependent gating.** S4D has no input-dependent gate of
  the LSTM kind; the entire mechanism is a fixed, well-initialised linear recurrence.
  That is a **data point against** the "the architecture needs input-dependent
  gating" hypothesis, in the same way Sutskever et al. show momentum suffices for
  long-range RNNs, but it is one architectural family, not a proof.
- **It is about SSMs, not about the byte stream.** The long-range-dependency tasks
  in LRA are not character- or byte-level language modelling with no tokenizer.
- **Its parameterisation is complex-valued and spectral.** TMT's `.decay` is real,
  scalar, per-coordinate. Importing "use complex `A`" or "use the S4 kernel"
  wholesale is a different model, not a tuning change.
- **It does not address the optimiser at all.** No Adam, no SNR, no learning rate.
  It cannot speak to the SNR blocker.
- The equivalence to S4 is a **limit-of-infinite-state-dimension** result. It is not
  a finite-size guarantee, and it is not a guarantee about the *recurrent* (as
  opposed to convolutional) instantiation.

## 4. Bearing on the three open questions

- **Noise floor vs over-large step?** No bearing.
- **Estimator vs optimiser?** No bearing. Its value is entirely on the
  architecture axis.
- **Input-dependent gating?** The most informative entry on this question. It shows
  a *non-gated* diagonal linear recurrent model achieving long-range-dependency
  results **when the decay spectrum is initialised well** — i.e. before adding
  gating, fix the parameterisation. TMT's 8–32 B horizon may be a
  parameterisation/initialisation failure rather than an architectural ceiling. Test
  the log-spaced initialisation *before* proposing gates.
