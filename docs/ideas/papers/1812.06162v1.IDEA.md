## 1. What they actually show

The **gradient noise scale** `B_simple` is a cheap statistic, measured with a
minimal-overhead procedure on a small batch, that estimates the batch size beyond
which extra samples per step stop buying loss reduction. `B_crit` is measured
separately, by fitting the compute-efficiency Pareto front of carefully-tuned full
training runs.

Verified content worth quoting:

- `B_simple` predicts `B_crit` **at the order-of-magnitude level** across MNIST,
  SVHN, CIFAR-10, ImageNet, Billion Word, Atari, Dota, and SVHN autoencoders, with
  `B_crit` spanning **20 → >10 million**.
- The noise scale **increases as loss decreases over a run**, and depends on model
  size "primarily through improved model performance" — not directly on parameter
  count.
- **The measurement is not free of the learning rate.** §3 / App. C: `B_simple`
  depends on the LR through the "temperature" of training, so it must be measured
  at each LR value of a schedule. Noise scale is comparable across well-tuned runs
  *when compared at equal loss*.
- §2.3 states candidly that the model is a **strictly local** account: "greedy
  optimization might perform poorly in the presence of bad local minima or when
  the landscape is ill-conditioned," in which case `B_crit` is *smaller* than the
  greedy estimate.
- They also fit an "iso-floss" band: `B_crit` is where compute efficiency drops
  below 50 % of optimal, at which point time efficiency is also ≈50 % of optimal.

## 2. What this means for TMT

**TMT's SNR ≈ 0.05 and SNR = 0.633 at frozen θ is a temperature measurement, not a
property of the landscape.** This paper's own §3/App. C says the noise scale is
LR-dependent, and `RESULTS.md`'s finding that the *same estimator* at a moving θ
reads 0.05 and at a frozen θ reads 0.633 is exactly that dependence: the iterate
moving every byte re-rolls the temperature. Read TMT's 0.05 as "the effective
noise temperature is high relative to the step being taken," which is the same
statement as "the step is too large" — the diagnosis `RESULTS.md` already
recorded. **The untested lever this paper nominates is a batch-size / accumulation
sweep**, and TMT has never run one.

Named test: at fixed data order, accumulate over `B ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64}`
mini-batches at a fixed number of parameter updates, and record (a) `B_simple` per
App. A.1's minimal-overhead procedure, (b) CE at matched *updates*, (c) CE at
matched *bytes*. If `B_simple` predicts where CE-vs-updates stops improving, the
accumulation experiment has a quantitative target rather than a guess. Judge
against the fixed ruler setting already used in the repo, not against training CE
alone.

## 3. What this paper does NOT support

- It says nothing about **recurrent** or **byte-level** models. Every experiment is
  a CNN, an RL policy, or an autoencoder. Its language-model arm (Billion Word) is
  an LSTM but is used only as a source of a batch-size number.
- It does **not** say "large batch fixes low SNR." It says `B_crit ≈ B_simple` and
  that beyond `B_crit` the run is per-step-limited. Accumulation beyond `B_crit`
  buys parallel efficiency and nothing else — the paper's own Fig. 6 shows runs at
  `B ≫ B_crit` behave similarly per optimization step.
- It does **not** license training TMT at a huge batch: with 4.48 M parameters and
  a ~0.9 GB corpus, TMT is orders of magnitude below the regime where this
  machinery is designed to operate. Accumulation is an *instrument* here (it buys
  an SNR measurement), not a proposed production setting.
- `B_simple` is not the noise floor of the loss. It is a statement about the
  marginal value of samples per step.

## 4. Bearing on the three open questions

- **Is the SNR blocker a noise floor or an over-large-step regime?** This paper
  supports **over-large-step**, and supplies the measurement that would separate
  them: `B_simple` as a function of LR. If the noise floor were intrinsic, `B_simple`
  would not move with LR.
- **Does the estimator or the optimiser matter?** Neither, on this evidence. The
  estimator change (accumulation) is the *diagnostic*; the optimiser is untouched.
- **Does the architecture need input-dependent gating?** No bearing whatsoever.
