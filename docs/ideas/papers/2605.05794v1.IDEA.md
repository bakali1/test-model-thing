# Paper notes — "Revealing Modular Gradient Noise Imbalance in LLMs" / MoLS (arXiv:2605.05794v1)

**Paper:** Ziqing Wen, Zhouyang Liu, Jiahuan Wang, Ping Luo, Li Shen, Dongsheng Li,
Tao Sun — *Revealing Modular Gradient Noise Imbalance in LLMs: Calibrating Adam via
Signal-to-Noise Ratio*, arXiv:2605.05794v1 [cs.LG, cs.AI], 7 May 2026.

**Source text:** `2605.05794v1.paper.txt`; `2605.05794v1.paper.html.gz`.

**Verification status: VERIFIED — the attributed claim is supported.** Abstract page
fetched 2026-10-05; title and authors match. The external gloss — *"module-level SNR
scaling for LLMs (MoLS)"* — is confirmed: the method is named **MoLS** =
**M**odule-wise Learning Rate Scaling via **SNR**, in the abstract and throughout
§§1, 3, 4.

---

## 1. The argument and the method

The claim is that Adam's **per-parameter** adaptivity is the wrong granularity:
LLMs are composed of heterogeneous modules, and the resulting **module-level
gradient SNR is imbalanced**, so some modules are systematically over-stepped
relative to their signal while others are under-stepped. MoLS:

1. estimates **module-level SNRs** during a short **calibration phase** (empirically
   module SNR magnitudes stabilise after the **first 1 %** of training steps, so a
   one-shot estimate at warm-up suffices);
2. applies the resulting **per-module learning-rate scaling factors** thereafter;
3. is **plug-and-play into Adam** — "instead of altering Adam's update rule or
   introducing computationally expensive approximations" — and also works with
   Adam-mini.

Reported: up to **1.06 PPL reduction** over Adam at 1.3 B, **~1.2–1.4× speedup** vs
Adam with tuned hyperparameters, **<2 % wall-clock overhead**, **no GPU memory
overhead** (SNR estimated on CPU), compatible with memory-efficient optimizers
(**1.53 PPL** reduction at 1.3 B with Adam-mini). Baselines include LAMB and
module-wise LR tuning.

Foundational dependency: MoLS builds on the correlation between Adam's updates and
gradient SNR established by **Orvieto & Gower (2025)** — cited in §1. MoLS is not
independent of that result.

## 2. What this means for TMT

MoLS is the one paper on the shelf that reframes the SNR problem at a granularity
TMT actually has: **per-module**, not per-parameter. TMT's 4,481,793 parameters are
not homogeneous either — most notably the 8,192 `.decay` coordinates (0.183 % of
the model) live on a different scale and a different rule, and the repo's own state
probe reads an 8192-dim state while the decay histogram is a per-coordinate
amplitude.

But **MoLS is a poor fit for TMT, and the mismatch is worth recording rather than
glossing:**

- MoLS's premise is *within-model heterogeneity between architecturally distinct
  modules* (attention vs MLP vs embeddings in a Transformer). TMT's architecture is
  uniform; there may be nothing to rebalance. If so, MoLS's premise is vacuous here
  — which is itself a testable prediction (measure per-module SNR in TMT; if all
  modules read the same, MoLS has nothing to do).
- MoLS's mechanism requires that **Adam's update magnitude actually tracks gradient
  SNR**. TMT has *measured the opposite*: `|update|/|m|` spread of **1.0020×** — the
  denominator is effectively a constant, so there is no per-coordinate or
  per-module SNR information in the update to rescale. **MoLS's precondition is
  violated in TMT.** Scaling a constant by a per-module factor is equivalent to
  scaling the learning rate.
- Scale: 60 M–7 B, LLaMA, C4/OpenWebText, seq len 256, batch 512, BF16. TMT is
  4.48 M, byte-level, ~0.9 GB.

Named test, cheap and negative-by-default: log per-module gradient SNR (and the
implied Adam update scale) for TMT's major module groups for ≥100 steps at a frozen
θ and while training. **Prediction:** SNR will be near-uniform across modules and the
update scale will be flat. If that holds, MoLS is not applicable and the finding —
"Adam's denominator carries no module- or parameter-level information in TMT" —
should be recorded as a *result*, which is more valuable than a borrowed method.

## 3. What this paper does NOT support

- **It does not support "Adam is the wrong optimiser."** It supports "Adam's
  granularity is wrong for heterogeneous Transformers." MoLS keeps Adam's update rule
  and only re-scales module learning rates.
- **It does not show MoLS helps where there is no module heterogeneity**, or at
  small scale. No experiment below 60 M.
- **It does not measure memory, horizons, decay parameters, or anything
  architectural.** Nothing maps onto TMT's 8–32 B probed horizon.
- **Its gains are PPL reductions of order 1 on 60M–7B LLaMA.** Nothing in the paper
  suggests the effect survives a 10⁴× parameter-count reduction, and the mechanism
  (module heterogeneity) does not obviously scale with size at all.
- **The SNR-estimation calibration is a hyperparameter-laden engineering step**
  (a warm-up fraction, a sampling procedure) even though the paper presents MoLS as
  "tuning-free." It removes *manual module LR tuning*, not all tuning.
- **It relies on Orvieto & Gower (2025)** for "Adam updates correlate with gradient
  SNR." That correlation is the whole foundation, and it is exactly what TMT's
  measurement contradicts. Treat MoLS as conditional on a premise TMT has falsified
  locally.
- No peer review; no journal reference.

## 4. Bearing on the three open questions

- **Noise floor vs over-large step?** No bearing — the granularity is wrong for TMT.
- **Estimator vs optimiser?** Optimiser, but via a route TMT cannot take (its
  precondition is locally false).
- **Input-dependent gating?** No bearing.
